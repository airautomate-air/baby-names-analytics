Every generation produces names that feel modern at birth and dated by middle age. Jennifer, Ashley, and Tiffany belong to this category. So do Crystal, Brittany, and Madison. Naming is full of traps.

But a small set of names have been in the top 100 every single decade for over a century. Not "classic." Not "old-fashioned." Actually used, consistently, by enough families in each generation to hold their position.

Here's who they are, with decade-by-decade data from the SSA.

---

The following names appear in the top 100 in every decade from the 1920s through the 2020s — eleven consecutive decades.

James was #1 in the 1940s and is #5 in the 2020s. It dipped to #17 in the 2000s and came back. The combination of biblical depth and royal/literary presence (six Scottish kings, James Joyce, James Baldwin, Henry James) gives it a durability that has outlasted every fashion cycle.

## Why do these names last?

**Biblical or classical roots.** Every name on the boys' list comes from the Hebrew Bible or New Testament. Elizabeth is Hebrew. Anna is Greek/Hebrew. Sarah is the matriarch. Names with religious roots have an institutional permanence — they're embedded in churches, synagogues, saints' days, and calendars in ways that fashion names aren't.

**Short, pronounceable, cross-cultural.** William, James, John, David, Daniel — all under three syllables, all spell-as-they-sound, all work in English, French, Spanish, Italian, and German without significant adjustment. International portability reduces vulnerability to any single cultural shift.

**Range of nicknames.** William → Will, Wills, Bill, Billy, Liam. James → Jim, Jimmy, Jamie. Elizabeth → Beth, Bess, Eliza, Lisa, Liz, Libby. Each name serves different eras through different short forms without the core name ever feeling dated.

**They're not associated with one specific generation.** Jennifer is associated with the 1970s–80s; Jessica with the late 1980s–early 90s. Elizabeth is associated with every era simultaneously — too diffuse to date. The same is true of James and John.
