*Bridgerton* (Netflix, premiered December 25, 2020) is one of the most-watched English-language Netflix series ever. Set in Regency-era London, it stars a large cast of Bridgerton siblings with conspicuously distinctive names — Daphne, Eloise, Hyacinth, Francesca, Anthony, Benedict, Colin, Gregory.

The naming effect is real but selective: some characters' names have climbed significantly; others have barely moved. And several Bridgerton names were already climbing before the show due to the vintage revival.

---

## Eloise — the biggest Bridgerton naming effect

**[Eloise](/name/eloise.html)** — Girls #64 (3,430 babies). Eloise Bridgerton is the fourth Bridgerton child — witty, bookish, feminist, determined not to marry. She is one of the fan-favorite characters in the series.

| Year | Rank | Babies |
|------|------|--------|
| 2018 | #166 | 1,743 |
| 2019 | #149 | 1,902 |
| 2020 | #137 | 1,969 |
| 2021 | #109 | 2,547 |
| 2022 | #86 | 2,904 |
| 2023 | #81 | 2,921 |
| 2024 | **#64** | **3,430** |

Eloise gained roughly 1,700 additional babies per year between 2018 and 2024 — jumping from #166 to #64. The acceleration begins in 2021 (the first full naming cycle after the December 2020 premiere). While Eloise was already climbing as part of the vintage-name revival, the post-2020 acceleration is clearly driven by the show.

Eloise is also the name of the beloved children's book character (Eloise at the Plaza, 1955) — giving it a literary-whimsical association independent of Bridgerton. See [skip-generation names](/blog/skip-generation-names.html).

| Year | Rank | Babies |
|------|------|--------|
| 2018 | #384 | 808 |
| 2019 | #435 | 734 |
| 2020 | #415 | 752 |
| 2021 | #289 | 1,102 |
| 2022 | #279 | 1,132 |
| 2023 | #241 | 1,265 |

Daphne's climb starts sharply in 2021 — almost exactly doubling from its pre-Bridgerton baseline. Daphne Bridgerton is a more traditional figure than Eloise (she marries in Season 1 and steps back from the spotlight), but the Season 1 focus on her character gave the name its initial boost.

Daphne is also a name from Greek mythology: Daphne was a naiad (water nymph) who was transformed into a laurel tree to escape the pursuit of Apollo. The mythological association gives Daphne an independent pedigree. See [mythological names](/blog/mythological-names.html).

## Violet — impossible to separate

**[Violet](/name/violet.html)** — Girls #15 (6,972 babies). Viscountess Violet Bridgerton is the family matriarch — warm, witty, determined to see all her children well-married. Violet is also the name of the color.

Violet's extraordinary rise (from #734 in 2000 to #15 in 2024) predates Bridgerton entirely — it was already at #43 in 2018 before the show premiered. The show may have given Violet additional visibility in 2020–2022, but the color-name trend and the vintage revival are clearly the primary drivers. See [color names](/blog/color-names.html).

## Penelope — already mainstream before Bridgerton

**[Penelope](/name/penelope.html)** — Girls #28 (5,741 babies). Penelope Featherington — revealed to be the gossip columnist Lady Whistledown in a Season 1 twist — becomes the central character of Season 3.

Penelope was already mainstream before the show:

| Year | Rank |
|------|------|
| 2015 | #34 |
| 2018 | #26 |
| 2020 | #25 |
| 2024 | **#28** |

Penelope was #34 in 2015 — long before Bridgerton — and has been in the mid-20s since. The show has kept it prominent but didn't drive its rise. Penelope is a name from Homer's *Odyssey* (Odysseus's faithful wife) that has been climbing as part of the vintage Greek-name revival.

## The Regency aesthetic

Beyond individual character names, Bridgerton has amplified an entire aesthetic cluster of Regency-era names:

- **Eloise** ✓ (character's name, clearly boosted)
- **Daphne** ✓ (character's name, clearly boosted)
- **Georgiana/Georgie** (background character)
- **Marina** (Season 1 character) — #640, stable
- **Prudence** (minor character) — #2,588, stable
- **Genevieve** (seamstress character) — #165, stable

The broader aesthetic is also reflected in the non-Bridgerton names that are climbing simultaneously:
- Anastasia, Arabella, Evangeline, Cecilia — see [long and elaborate names](/blog/long-baby-names.html)
- Charlotte, Eleanor, Beatrice — the Regency-era royals

Bridgerton works with the vintage naming revival rather than driving it. The show made these names *more visible* to parents who were already inclined toward them.

## The Anthony problem

**[Anthony](/name/anthony.html)** — Boys #44 (6,326 babies). Viscount Anthony Bridgerton is the eldest son and protagonist of Season 2. Anthony is a top-50 boys' name that has been declining since 2005:

| Year | Rank |
|------|------|
| 2000 | #25 |
| 2010 | #31 |
| 2018 | #38 |
| 2024 | **#44** |

Anthony has continued to decline *despite* Bridgerton. The Latin/Italian *Antonius* root is a common Catholic name with centuries of history; Anthony was always more mainstream than exotic. The show's Season 2 focus on Anthony (and his romance with Kate Sharma) hasn't reversed the name's long decline.

This is the male-name asymmetry: Bridgerton has clearly boosted female character names (Eloise, Daphne, Francesca) but has shown no measurable effect on male character names (Anthony, Benedict, Colin, Gregory are all declining or flat).

## Benedict and Colin — minimal effect

**[Benedict](/name/benedict.html)** — Boys #913 (254 babies). Benedict Bridgerton is the Season 3 subplot character, Season 4 protagonist. Has ticked up slightly from #985 (2018) to #913 (2024). Benedict is still extremely rare in the US — the papal and monastic associations (Saint Benedict, Pope Benedict) may make it feel too religious for some parents.

**[Colin](/name/colin.html)** — Boys #334 (1,035 babies). Colin Bridgerton is Penelope's love interest and Season 3 co-protagonist. Has been declining: #249 (2018) to #334 (2024). Despite prominent Season 3 placement, Colin continues its long slow decline. The name was popular in the 1980s-1990s as a British import and is now reading as a previous generation's name. See [generational names](/blog/generational-names.html).

## What Bridgerton actually did

Bridgerton's naming effect is most visible in a specific category: distinctive female characters with unusual, classical names that were already climbing as vintage revivals. Eloise and Daphne fit this template perfectly — they were rising names that the show gave a significant additional push.

Names that were already mainstream before the show (Penelope, Charlotte, Violet) show no measurable Bridgerton effect. Names without independent appeal (Hyacinth, Edwina, Cressida) remain essentially unused.

The pattern mirrors other prestige period dramas:
- *Downton Abbey* (2010–2015) boosted Violet, Mary, Sybil, and Edith
- *The Crown* (2016–present) kept Elizabeth and Margaret in the cultural conversation
- Bridgerton is doing the same for Eloise, Daphne, and Francesca

All three shows work with names that were already interesting to parents who appreciate historical or vintage aesthetics. Prestige period drama is the tastemaker for vintage naming — not the inventor.
