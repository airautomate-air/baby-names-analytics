The year was 1974. Nixon had just resigned over Watergate. The OPEC oil embargo was squeezing American drivers. *The Godfather Part II* was in cinemas. About 3.2 million babies were born in the US, and a striking number of them were named Michael or Jennifer.

## What stands out

**The boys' list has aged remarkably well.** Eight of the 15 are still inside the top 100 today. James and William are back in the top 5. Michael, despite being the defining name of the whole postwar generation, is still top 20. The biblical core — James, John, Daniel, Joseph — was never really going anywhere.

**The girls' list is a different story.** Jennifer (#1 in 1974) is now #831. Lisa is #932. Tammy, Tracy, and Dawn are practically out of use entirely. The 1970s girls' list was driven by names that had sharp, concentrated peaks — they felt modern and fresh in their moment and dated extremely fast.

**Jennifer's fall is the steepest in naming history.** In 1974, roughly 1 in 50 girls born in the US was named Jennifer. By 2024 it's barely top 800. No other name has fallen so far so fast from such a height. There's a specific sociological pattern here: Jennifer went from distinctive → ubiquitous → avoided, all within 30 years.

**The 1970s boys that aren't coming back:** Brian, Kevin, Jeffrey. These names don't have the classical depth that protects James or John, but they also don't have the 100-year distance that makes an Edwardian name feel fresh. They're in the awkward middle — too recent to be vintage, too dated to feel current.

**Which might revive?** The names best positioned for a comeback from the 1974 list:
- **[Rebecca](/name/rebecca.html)** — biblical, never fully out, with a classic short form (Becca/Becky)
- **[Nicole](/name/nicole.html)** — still active, just quieter
- **[Kelly](/name/kelly.html)** — short, clean, unisex potential
- **[Amy](/name/amy.html)** — simple, friendly, one syllable; could follow the Ruth/Ruth-revival pattern
