The four years between 2020 and 2024 reshuffled the girl-name chart almost as much as they reshuffled the rest of life. These are the ten names that fell furthest — all of them top-300 girl picks in 2020, all of them well outside the top 350 today.

## The list

1. **[Alexa](/name/alexa.html)** — from #230 in 2020 to **#806 in 2024** (−576). The biggest fall on the list, and it has an obvious culprit: a smart-speaker named Alexa moved into millions of US homes. Parents stopped naming their daughters after the family appliance.
2. **[Aubree](/name/aubree.html)** — from #156 to **#408** (−252). The "-ee" ending wave (Aubree, Bailee, Marlee) is receding; the parents picking variant spellings have moved on to other vowel sounds.
3. **[Mckenzie](/name/mckenzie.html)** — from #281 to **#493** (−212). All the Mc- and Mac- prefixed surname-names are sliding together.
4. **[Brooklynn](/name/brooklynn.html)** — from #262 to **#468** (−206). The double-N spelling is fading faster than plain Brooklyn.
5. **[Alyssa](/name/alyssa.html)** — from #199 to **#399** (−200). A 1990s/2000s mainstay continues its long graceful descent.
6. **[Raegan](/name/raegan.html)** — from #259 to **#458** (−199). Reagan/Raegan spelled either way is sliding; the Raegan variant is sliding faster.
7. **[Cali](/name/cali.html)** — from #296 to **#495** (−199). Short geography names rose hard in the 2010s and are now picking the next destinations.
8. **[Taylor](/name/taylor.html)** — from #162 to **#353** (−191). One of the great cross-gender surname-names of the '90s, still trending down despite the cultural megastar attached to it.
9. **[Trinity](/name/trinity.html)** — from #225 to **#407** (−182). Virtue and religious names ran hot in the 2000s and are now cooling.
10. **[Finley](/name/finley.html)** — from #201 to **#365** (−164). Finley peaked around 2018–2020 for girls and is now drifting; the boys' Finley is holding steadier.

## What's the pattern?

Three themes run through the list:

- **Variant spellings are losing ground.** Aubree, Mckenzie, Brooklynn, Raegan — four of ten. The 2010s appetite for distinguishing your child via spelling has clearly cooled.
- **2000s-era word and virtue names are sliding.** Trinity, Cali, Taylor — names that anchored that era are giving way to longer, softer, more vintage choices (Charlotte, Eleanor, Hazel — see our [vintage comeback post](/blog/vintage-girl-names-comeback.html)).
- **A few have clear external causes.** Alexa is the clearest cultural collision in modern name data. Once Amazon launched the device in 2014, the name's slide accelerated; the post-2020 nosedive is the long tail of that decision.
