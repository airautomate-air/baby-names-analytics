The Arabic and Persian layers of the US chart have grown faster than almost any other origin category over the past two decades. Layla is a top-40 girl name. Amir and Adam are top-100 boys. And the long tail is full of names — Yara, Nour, Rayan, Idris — that simply did not appear in American data in 2000.

## Persian-origin names

The Persian layer is smaller in volume but distinctive. Many of these names share Sasanid or Zoroastrian roots, and several have become global through Persian poetry and history.

Boys:

- **[Cyrus](/name/cyrus.html)** — #254. *Kūrush*, possibly "sun" — the Achaemenid king.
- **[Kian](/name/kian.html)** — #416. From *Kayanian*, a legendary Persian dynasty — "king" or "noble".
- **[Damon](/name/damon.html)** — #454. Greek-Persian crossover — "to tame" in Greek; a Persian historical name.
- **[Darius](/name/darius.html)** — #746. *Dārayavauš*, "holding firm to the good" — three Persian emperors.
- **[Arman](/name/arman.html)** — #1,293. "Wish" or "aspiration".

Girls:

- **[Layla](/name/layla.html)** — also Persian via the Layla and Majnun epic
- **[Jasmine](/name/jasmine.html)** — #199. From *yāsamīn*, the flower
- **[Leyla](/name/leyla.html)** — #601. Variant of Layla
- **[Soraya](/name/soraya.html)** — #913. *Soraya* — the Pleiades constellation
- **[Yasmin](/name/yasmin.html)** — #917. Variant of Jasmine
- **[Roxanne](/name/roxanne.html)** — #1,275. *Roxana*, "bright" — Alexander the Great's wife

## What's driving it

The growth is mostly demographic: Arab-American and Iranian-American families have become a meaningful share of births in major metros, and second-generation parents are no longer trimming names to fit Anglo expectations. But two trends affect the broader chart too:

1. **Soft, two-syllable, vowel-rich names work universally.** Layla, Sara, Mira, Nora, Yara, Lina — these read as Arabic, Persian, Hebrew, *and* generic-modern at once. They cross.
2. **Single-syllable boys' names are open territory.** Amir, Adam, Omar, Ali, Zayn — three or four letters, easy to spell, no awkward consonants. The chart is hungry for short boys' names. Arabic naming is supplying them.
