The most popular names in the United States have a secret: many of them are warrior and protector names from Germanic and Old English traditions. Liam is #1 and means "resolute protector." William is #10 and shares the same root. Henry is #6 and means "home ruler." Charlotte is #4 and traces to the same Germanic *Karl* that means free man and warrior.

---

## The *wil + helm* family: Liam, William, and variants

The Old High German name *Willahelm* combined two words:
- *willo*: will, determination, desire
- *helm*: helmet, protection, armor

Together: "one who desires protection" or "determined protector" — a warrior who wears the armor of resolve. This name entered English as **William**, crossed to Ireland as **Uilliam**, and shortened in both countries to the form we now have as **Liam**.

### Liam — #1 (22,164 babies)

**[Liam](/name/liam.html)** — Irish short form of *Uilliam* (the Irish form of William). The most popular boys' name in the United States for the past six years. Liam Hemsworth, Liam Neeson, Liam Gallagher. At #1 with 22,164 babies in 2024.

### William — #10 (10,596 babies)

**[William](/name/william.html)** — Old High German *Willahelm* directly. William the Conqueror introduced it to England in 1066; it has been in the English top 5 for roughly 900 years, with brief breaks. See [timeless names](/blog/timeless-names.html). At #10.

Together, Liam (#1, 22,164 babies) and William (#10, 10,596 babies) account for 32,760 babies in 2024 under the same root name — more than any other etymological family.

## The *haim + ric* family: Henry and variants

Old High German *Haimric*: *haim* (home, estate) + *ric* (ruler, power, rich). "Ruler of the home" — a name for a lord or estate-keeper. In its medieval French form *Henri* → English *Henry*, it became one of the dominant names of royalty.

### Henry — #6 (11,547 babies)

**[Henry](/name/henry.html)** — eight English kings bore this name. Henry IV, Henry V (Shakespeare's heroic warrior king), Henry VIII. Prince Harry (Henry Charles Albert David) carries the name as well. At #6.

### Harry — #778 (322 babies)

**[Harry](/name/harry.html)** — medieval English pet form of Henry, evolved through the Middle English *Herry*. Harry Potter; Prince Harry; Harry Styles. Despite the celebrity associations, Harry is surprisingly rare in the US (#778) — far more common in the UK, where it has been top-5 for decades. See [royal names](/blog/royal-baby-names.html).

### Harvey — #245 (1,441 babies)

**[Harvey](/name/harvey.html)** — Old French *Hervé*, from Breton *haerv* (battle) + *viu* (blazing, active): "battle-worthy" or "blazing in battle." Harvey has been climbing rapidly — it was #326 in 2023 and is now #245. The British vintage revival has crossed the Atlantic. See [fastest-rising 2024](/blog/fastest-rising-2024.html).

## The *Karl/Charles* family: Charles, Charlotte, Carlos

Old High German *Karl*: from Latin *Carolus*, from a Germanic root meaning "free man" — in the Medieval Germanic context, a free man was one who could bear arms: a warrior. Charlemagne (Karl der Grosse, Charles the Great) made this the dominant Western royal name.

### Charles — #51 (5,593 babies)

**[Charles](/name/charles.html)** — the original English form. Charles I, Charles II, Prince Charles (King Charles III). At #51.

### Charlotte — #4 (12,552 babies)

**[Charlotte](/name/charlotte.html)** — French feminine form of Charles, from *Karl*. Charlotte is the most successful warrior-root girls' name in current data — at #4 with 12,552 babies. Princess Charlotte (born 2015) accelerated a rise that was already underway. The "free/noble man" meaning has become entirely invisible; Charlotte now reads as purely elegant and British.

### Carlos — #135 (2,614 babies)

**[Carlos](/name/carlos.html)** — Spanish form of Charles. At #135 in 2024 data.

### Caroline — #92 (2,720 babies)

**[Caroline](/name/caroline.html)** — Latin feminine of *Carolus*. Caroline Kennedy, *Sweet Caroline* (Neil Diamond). At #92.

## Other warrior and protector names

### Barrett — #186 (1,984 babies)

**[Barrett](/name/barrett.html)** — Old German *Beroald*: *bero* (bear) + *wald* (power/rule): "bear power" or "rule of the bear." The bear was a symbol of fierce protective strength in Germanic tradition. At #186 and climbing.

### Emmett — #119 (3,001 babies)

**[Emmett](/name/emmett.html)** — from Old German *Ermintrude* or *Ermen* (universal) — one of the universal/powerful names. See [vintage boy names](/blog/vintage-boy-names-comeback.html).

### Milo — #120 (2,988 babies)

**[Milo](/name/milo.html)** — from Old High German *Milo*, related to *mild* (mild/peaceful, literally "soft warrior") — or possibly from Slavic *milu* (gracious). Milo of Croton was the legendary Greek wrestler. At #120.

### Gerald — #1,167 (178 babies)

**[Gerald](/name/gerald.html)** — Old High German *Gerwald*: *ger* (spear) + *wald* (rule): "rule by the spear" — a warrior-ruler name. Gerald Ford, Jerry Garcia. Now rare at #1,167 — the classic mid-century name that hasn't yet revived.

### Duncan — #1,103 (194 babies)

**[Duncan](/name/duncan.html)** — Scottish Gaelic *Donnchadh*: *donn* (dark, brown) + *cath* (warrior, battle): "dark warrior." King Duncan in *Macbeth*. At #1,103 — rare in the US.

### Matilda — #410 (769 babies)

**[Matilda](/name/matilda.html)** — the leading girls' warrior-name. Old High German *Mahthildis*: *maht* (might, power) + *hild* (battle): "mighty in battle." Empress Matilda (the 12th-century English claimant), *Roald Dahl*'s Matilda, *Waltzing Matilda*. At #410. See [names meaning strength](/blog/names-meaning-strength.html).

## The Germanic warrior-name structure

The Old Germanic tradition built names from two-element compounds, mixing warrior vocabulary:

| First element | Meaning | Examples |
|--------------|---------|---------|
| *wil/willo* | will/desire | William, Liam |
| *haim* | home/estate | Henry, Harry |
| *Karl/ger* | free man/spear | Charles, Gerald, Garrett |
| *bero/bar* | bear | Bernard, Barrett |
| *maht/mago* | might/power | Matilda, Milo |
| *hild* | battle | Matilda, Hilda, Brunhilde |
| *wald* | rule/power | Gerald, Aldric, Oswald |
| *helm* | helmet/protection | William, Anselm |
| *hard* | brave/hardy | Everett, Leonard, Bernard |

This system produced hundreds of European given names. Most of them are now rare or obsolete — but the most successful ones (William, Henry, Charles, Matilda) survived because they were adopted by royalty and thus stayed in the naming pool.

## The hidden warrior at the top

The most important insight from this data: the two most popular boys' names in the United States — **Liam** (#1) and **William** (#10) — are the same name, and that name means "resolute protector" with a helmet of armor. Charlotte (#4) for girls traces to "free man/warrior." Henry (#6) is "home ruler."

The modern preference for these names isn't consciously about their warrior meaning — parents choosing Liam in 2024 are not thinking about helmets and battle-readiness. But the meaning embedded in these names goes back 1,200 years, when *Willahelm* was literally a name for a noble warrior ready to protect his family and estate.
