American religious observance has declined. Church attendance is down. The number of adults identifying as Christian has fallen from 90% in 1976 to about 63% in 2024.

And yet Old Testament names are surging.

**Isaiah** (#36) and **Ezekiel** (#66) are both in the top 100. **Malachi** (#136) is rising fast. **Silas** (#82) is at its highest rank in 130 years. **Levi** (#15) is in the top 20. **Judah** is in the top 200. **Amos**, **Obadiah**, **Gideon**, and **Ezra** are all climbing.

The Old Testament name wave is real, broad, and driven by forces that go well beyond religious affiliation.

## Levi: the patriarch in the top 20

**[Levi](/name/levi.html)** — Boys #15, 13,126 babies in 2024.

Levi was the third son of Jacob and founder of the tribe of Levi — the priestly tribe from which Moses descended. It's also an American denim brand. In 2024 it's the 15th most popular boys name in America.

| Year | Rank | Babies |
|------|------|--------|
| 1994 | #264 | 1,108 |
| 2000 | #193 | 1,821 |
| 2004 | #138 | 3,046 |
| 2010 | #75 | 5,776 |
| 2014 | #42 | 8,006 |
| 2018 | #24 | 11,209 |
| 2022 | **#13** | **13,412** |
| 2024 | #15 | 13,126 |

Levi is near its peak and essentially holding. It benefits from being short (two syllables), having a denim/outdoors cultural association alongside the biblical one, and feeling both old and modern simultaneously. Evangelical Christians, secular families, and Jewish families have all embraced it.

## Isaiah: the major prophet returns

**[Isaiah](/name/isaiah.html)** — Boys #36, 7,982 babies in 2024.

Isaiah is the longest book in the Old Testament — a prophet of major theological importance in Judaism, Christianity, and Islam. The name was barely used in mainstream America until the 1990s.

| Year | Rank | Babies |
|------|------|--------|
| 1994 | #162 | 2,042 |
| 2004 | #65 | 5,971 |
| 2010 | #44 | 8,210 |
| 2014 | #41 | 8,404 |
| 2020 | #38 | 7,812 |

Isaiah rose steadily through the 1990s and 2000s and has plateaued in the mid-30s to 40s range for the past decade. It's one of the most established of the prophet names — at two decades in the top 50, it's starting to feel like a classic rather than a trend.

## Ezekiel: the prophet with the nickname

**[Ezekiel](/name/ezekiel.html)** — Boys #66, 5,319 babies in 2024.

Ezekiel was a major prophet of the Babylonian captivity period — the source of the "valley of dry bones" and the chariot-throne vision. It's an unusual choice to reach the top 100, but the nickname Zeke makes it practical.

| Year | Rank | Babies |
|------|------|--------|
| 1994 | #348 | 733 |
| 2004 | #272 | 1,108 |
| 2014 | #157 | 2,384 |
| 2018 | #87 | 4,648 |
| 2022 | **#64** | **5,428** |
| 2024 | #66 | 5,319 |

Ezekiel's rapid rise (from #272 to #66 in 20 years) surprised many naming observers. The Zeke nickname removes the length barrier, and the name itself has a bold, unusual quality that parents seeking a distinctive biblical name appreciate. In sports, Ezekiel Elliott (NFL running back) kept the name in cultural view through the 2010s-2020s.

Malachi is holding in the low 100s-130s. The three-syllable flow (MAL-a-kye) is smooth and confident. ## The minor prophets making moves: Amos and Gideon

**[Amos](/name/amos.html)** — Boys #430, 771 babies in 2024.

Amos was a shepherd-prophet from Tekoa who preached against social injustice — one of the earliest advocates for economic equality in recorded history. The name has a simple, two-syllable sound that fits perfectly with the current vintage-revival aesthetic.

| Year | Rank | Babies |

| 1994 | #719 | 266 |
| 2010 | #678 | 333 |
| 2014 | #656 | 361 |
| 2020 | #493 | 594 |
| 2024 | **#430** | **771** |

Amos has nearly tripled in count from 2014 to 2024. The TV show *Justified* (Amos character) and folk/indie music's embrace of biblical names have contributed. Amos is still early in its climb.

**[Gideon](/name/gideon.html)** — Boys #355, 916 babies in 2024.

Gideon led 300 soldiers to victory over 135,000 Midianites in the book of Judges — one of the great underdog stories in scripture. As a name it has gravitas and narrative weight.

## Why Old Testament names beyond religion?

The rise of prophet and patriarch names among secular American families reveals something important: these names are succeeding on their own terms, independent of their religious origins.

**1. They're genuinely old.** Isaiah, Ezekiel, Gideon — these are 2,500+ year old names. That antiquity signals permanence and weight in ways that invented names simply can't.

**2. They're rare but recognizable.** Unlike made-up unique names, Old Testament names are unusual enough to be distinctive but recognized enough to be pronounceable.

**3. They have built-in meaning.** Every biblical name comes with a story, a meaning, a lineage. Parents who want names that *mean something* find the prophets irresistible.

**4. They're not generationally associated.** Unlike Michael (1960s-70s), Jennifer (1970s-80s), or Ashley (1980s-90s), Ezekiel and Malachi don't belong to any living generation's parents. They can be claimed fresh.
