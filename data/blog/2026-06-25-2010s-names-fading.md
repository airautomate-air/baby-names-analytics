Baby names from the 1970s and 1980s took 30–40 years to fall. Jennifer was #1 from 1970 to 1984 and didn't drop out of the top 200 until the early 2010s. Crystal peaked in the early 1980s and took two decades to fade.

The 2010s names are moving faster.

Alexa was #50 in 2010. It peaked at #32 in 2015. By 2024 it was #806. That is a collapse of 774 places in nine years — faster than anything comparable in the historical record. Alexis, another top-20 name in 2010, is now outside the top 500.

---

## The Alexa collapse

**[Alexa](/name/alexa.html)** tells the clearest story in the 2024 SSA data.

| Year | Rank | Babies |
|------|------|--------|
| 2010 | #50 | 5,043 |
| 2013 | #60 | 4,106 |
| 2014 | #64 | 4,249 |
| 2015 | #32 | 6,053 |
| 2016 | #51 | 4,775 |
| 2017 | #66 | 3,896 |
| 2018 | #90 | 3,067 |
| 2019 | #139 | 2,002 |
| 2020 | #230 | 1,284 |
| 2021 | #439 | 708 |
| 2022 | #535 | 577 |
| 2023 | #604 | 492 |
| 2024 | #806 | 348 |

Amazon launched its Echo smart speaker in November 2014, using "Alexa" as the wake word. In 2015, the first full year of widespread Echo adoption, Alexa the baby name rose briefly — from #64 to #32 — as the positive association was still dominant. Then, as "Alexa" became the name people shouted at a speaker in their kitchen, the baby name collapsed. The timing is precise: the drop begins in 2016 and accelerates every year through 2024.

In 2010, 5,043 babies were named Alexa. In 2024, 348 were. A 93% drop in fourteen years.

## The other fast faders

Alexa is the most dramatic case, but it is not alone.

| Name | 2010 rank | 2015 rank | 2020 rank | 2024 rank |
|------|-----------|-----------|-----------|-----------|
| Alexa | #50 | #32 | #230 | #806 |
| Alexis | #16 | #85 | #309 | #506 |
| Makayla | #43 | #143 | #325 | #542 |
| Kayla | #49 | #115 | #216 | #336 |
| Taylor | #36 | #77 | #162 | #353 |
| Lauren | #58 | #119 | #232 | #351 |
| Alyssa | #21 | #93 | #199 | #399 |
| Kaylee | #35 | #61 | #117 | #220 |

**[Alexis](/name/alexis.html)** was #16 in 2010 — a top-20 name given to over 8,000 girls that year. By 2024 it is #506, with fewer than 600 babies. Like many names with the -lex- or Alex- prefix, it has become associated with a specific late-1990s to 2000s aesthetic. It also shares some of the fallout from the Alexa/Amazon problem — both names invoke the same phonetic associations now.

**[Makayla](/name/makayla.html)** was #43 in 2010; now #542. Makayla (and its variant Mikayla, Michaela) was part of a wave of phonetically altered spellings — Michael → Makayla — that were fashionable in the late 1990s and 2000s. These alternate-spelling names have aged particularly fast.

**[Taylor](/name/taylor.html)** was #36; now #353. Taylor's fall is not because Taylor Swift made the name unusual — Swift was already famous in 2010. The issue is generational: Taylor peaked for girls in 1993 (#8) and for boys in 1994 (#10). The 2010 rank was already part of a long decline that has continued.

## The boys' side

Boys' 2010s names have faded more slowly, but the pattern is visible.

| Name | 2010 rank | 2015 rank | 2020 rank | 2024 rank |
|------|-----------|-----------|-----------|-----------|
| Jayden | #4 | #20 | #37 | #59 |
| Gavin | #38 | #70 | #142 | #255 |
| Brandon | #44 | #83 | #165 | #230 |
| Brayden | #40 | #61 | #111 | #190 |
| Tyler | #34 | #81 | #130 | #191 |
| Justin | #50 | #109 | #166 | #199 |

**[Jayden](/name/jayden.html)** was the #4 boys' name in 2010 — 17,200 babies. In 2024 it is #59, with 5,134 babies. That is a 70% drop in fourteen years. Jayden falls into the broader [-aiden family](/blog/aiden-names.html) (Aiden, Kayden, Brayden), a phonetic fashion that peaked around 2010 and has been retreating since.

**[Gavin](/name/gavin.html)** and **[Brandon](/name/brandon.html)** represent a different category: mainstream mid-tier names from the 2000s that have no special phonetic hook but have aged out of fashion. Gavin (#38 in 2010) is now #255; Brandon (#44) is now #230. These were solid, unremarkable names for their time. They are now associated with a specific cohort — the children born in the late 1990s and 2000s who are now young adults.

## Why faster?

There are several reasons the 2010s names are cycling out more quickly than their predecessors.

**Social media and faster taste cycles.** Name trends accelerate alongside pop culture cycles. A name that feels dated now gets identified as dated quickly. The feedback loop between cultural references and naming choices has shortened.

**The Amazon Alexa effect is not unique to Alexa.** Any name with a strong commercial or technological association can be compromised in a way that simply wasn't possible before the era of branded AI assistants. Alexa is the clearest example, but the broader dynamic — technology and branding competing with names — is new.

**Alternate spellings age faster.** Makayla, Kaylee, Alexis — names with non-traditional spellings or that emerged from phonetic creativity rather than etymology — tend to be more tightly associated with the specific era that created them. They have no historical anchor to fall back on.

The names that were top 50 in 2010 and have held their position or gained ground are mostly names with historical depth: Emma, Olivia, Sophia, Ava for girls; James, Henry, Oliver, William for boys. The lasting names in 2024 are, almost without exception, names that were also used in the 19th century.
