Every year, one boy's name sits at the very top of the Social Security Administration's list. The names that reach #1 aren't just popular — they define an era. Here's the full history of how the throne has changed hands since 1900.

## The full list

## What the data tells us

**John's 24-year run (1900–1923)** reflects just how dominant that name was across the English-speaking world in the early twentieth century. It wasn't just a first name — it was almost the default. One in every ten or twelve boys born in those decades was named John.

**Michael's extraordinary 44-year reign** (1954–59, then 1961–1998) is the real anomaly. Interrupted only by David in 1960, Michael held #1 for nearly half a century. The name peaked in 1957 with more than 92,000 boys — a figure no name has come close to since the US population has grown but naming has diversified.

**Jacob's fourteen years (1999–2012)** marked the pivot away from mid-century classics toward Old Testament biblical names. Ethan, Noah, and Jacob all surged together; Jacob simply led the pack the longest.

**Noah's brief reign (2013–2016)** felt like a changing of the guard — the post-Jacob era, quieter in sound, more ancient in feel. Four years was enough to make it feel definitive.

**Liam has now led every year since 2017**, making it the first Irish-origin name to dominate the US charts. It's associated with a generation of Liam Neesons and One Direction Liams, but the numbers have taken on a life of their own. In 2024, more than 22,000 boys were named Liam — the highest total since Michael in the 1980s.

## What comes next?

No name stays at #1 forever. The names waiting closest behind Liam in 2024 are **Noah**, **Oliver**, and **James** — a mix of old testament, botanical, and royal-classic that suggests the next era could look very different from the Irish surge that brought Liam to the top.
