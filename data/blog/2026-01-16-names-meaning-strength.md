Names with roots in strength, power, and endurance have always been popular — strength is one of the oldest naming intentions across cultures. In 2024, several of the most popular names in the US have "strong" roots, often hidden beneath their familiar sounds.

---

## Girls Names Meaning Strength

### Valentina — #47 (4,438 babies)

**[Valentina](/name/valentina.html)** — Latin *valens* (participle of *valere*, to be strong, to be well). Valentine's Day comes from the same root: *valere* means strong health and vigor. Valentina is the Italian/Spanish feminine form — Valentine's Day's underlying meaning is strength, not just love. At #47, Valentina is the most popular strength-name for girls in 2024.

### Audrey — #82 (2,993 babies)

**[Audrey](/name/audrey.html)** — Old English *Æðelþryð*: *æðel* (noble) + *þryð* (strength). This is the oldest English word for strength still in active naming use. Saint Æthelthryth (later simplified to Audrey) was a 7th-century Anglo-Saxon queen renowned for her valor. Audrey Hepburn made the name elegant rather than medieval. At #82 and climbing.

### Freya — #160 (1,877 babies)

**[Freya](/name/freya.html)** — Old Norse, from *Freyja*, the Norse goddess associated with love, fertility, war, and strength. Freya is one of the principal Norse deities; her name comes from *freyja* (woman/lady), but her character is defined by battle-strength. At #160 and climbing steadily.

### Valerie — #147 (1,985 babies)

**[Valerie](/name/valerie.html)** — French form of Valeria, from the same Latin *valere* root as Valentina. Valerie is the more common but less glamorous relative of Valentina. At #147, it's near the mid-chart.

### Matilda — #410 (769 babies)

**[Matilda](/name/matilda.html)** — Old High German *Mahthildis*: *maht* (might, strength) + *hild* (battle). A perfectly etymological strength-name: it literally means "mighty in battle." Empress Matilda (medieval English claimant), *Roald Dahl*'s Matilda, the Australian folk song "Waltzing Matilda." At #410 — the same vintage revival territory as Hazel and Mabel. Short form Tilly is climbing independently.

### Astrid — #383 (818 babies)

**[Astrid](/name/astrid.html)** — Old Norse *Ástríðr*: *áss* (god) + *fríðr* (beautiful). Not strictly strength, but divine power and beauty — the Norse tradition doesn't always separate these. Several Scandinavian queens have been named Astrid. At #383.

### Ingrid — #1,092 (225 babies)

**[Ingrid](/name/ingrid.html)** — Old Norse *Ingríðr*: *Ing* (the Norse fertility god) + *fríðr* (beautiful/beloved). Ingrid Bergman. Still rare in the US at #1,092, but present.

### Andrea — #186 (1,624 babies)

**[Andrea](/name/andrea.html)** — Greek *andreía*, the noun for manliness, valor, courage — the same root as Andrew. In classical Greek ethics, *andreia* was the virtue of courage (listed by Aristotle alongside justice, temperance, and prudence). The feminine form has been used in English since the Middle Ages. At #186.

## Boys Names Meaning Strength

### Ethan — #25 (8,170 babies)

**[Ethan](/name/ethan.html)** — Hebrew *Étan*: firm, strong, enduring. Ethan appears in the Hebrew Bible as Ethan the Ezrahite, noted for his wisdom. The name means literal physical and moral firmness — not just muscular strength but stability and reliability. Ethan Allen (Revolutionary War hero) is the most famous American bearer. At #25.

### Gabriel — #43 (6,379 babies)

**[Gabriel](/name/gabriel.html)** — Hebrew *Gavri'el*: *gever* (man, warrior) + *El* (God) — "God is my strength" or "man of God." Gabriel is the archangel who brings God's messages to humanity — the herald of divine power. At #43, Gabriel has been inside the top 50 for over twenty years.

### Ezekiel — #54 (5,390 babies)

**[Ezekiel](/name/ezekiel.html)** — Hebrew *Yechezq'el*: *chazaq* (to strengthen) + *El* (God) — "God strengthens." Ezekiel is the Hebrew prophet who received the famous vision of the divine chariot (the merkavah) — a vision of overwhelming divine power. At #54, Ezekiel is one of the most popular explicitly theological strength-names.

Short form: Zeke (classic American frontier short form).

### Andrew — #68 (4,772 babies)

**[Andrew](/name/andrew.html)** — Greek *Andréas*, from *andreía* (manliness, valor, courage). Same root as Andrea. Andrew was one of Jesus' first disciples. Saint Andrew is the patron saint of Scotland, Russia, and Greece. At #68, Andrew has been in the American top 100 for nearly the entire history of SSA data.

### Everett — #85 (3,947 babies)

**[Everett](/name/everett.html)** — Old High German *Eberhart*: *ebur* (boar) + *hard* (brave, hardy). The boar was the symbol of courage and fierce strength in Germanic tradition. Everett has been climbing since around 2010 — it was #378 in 2000 and is now #85.

### Griffin — #223 (1,609 babies)

**[Griffin](/name/griffin.html)** — Welsh *Gruffudd*: *grip* (strong) + *udd* (lord) — "strong lord" or "lord of strength." The griffin is also the mythological creature with the body of a lion and head of an eagle — combining the two strongest animals. At #223 and climbing.

### Ezra — #13 (8,774 babies)

**[Ezra](/name/ezra.html)** — Hebrew *Ezra*, from *azár* (to help, to strengthen). Ezra the prophet "strengthened the hand" of those returning from Babylonian exile. At #13, Ezra is one of the defining boy names of the 2020s.

### Conrad — #470 (654 babies)

**[Conrad](/name/conrad.html)** — Old High German *Kuonrâd*: *kuon* (bold, experienced) + *rât* (counsel/strength). Conrad is the quiet German strength-name that hasn't crossed into mainstream use. Joseph Conrad (the novelist) and Conrad Hilton are the American reference points. At #470.

### Magnus — #749 (340 babies)

**[Magnus](/name/magnus.html)** — Latin *magnus*, great/large (in the sense of powerful, not just size). Magnus was used by Scandinavian royalty and church figures. Magnus Carlsen (the world chess champion) has kept the name visible. At #749 — rare but clearly placing it in the distinctive category.

## Names that sound powerful without meaning strength

Several popular names *feel* strong without having strength etymology:

- **Leo** (#24) — Latin for lion (fierce, not strong etymologically)
- **Theodore** (#4) — Greek, "gift of God" (not strength, but divine gift)
- **Atlas** (#101) — Greek mythological titan who held up the sky
- **Ares** (#295) — Greek god of war
- **Ajax** (rare) — Greek hero name, possibly "of the earth"
- **Maximus** (#330) — Latin *maximus*, greatest
- **Victor** (#214) — Latin, conqueror

These are "strength-adjacent" names that communicate power through their mythological or warrior associations rather than etymological meaning.

## The *val-* and *ard/hard* families

Two Germanic etymology clusters produce many strength names:

**The *val-* family** (from Latin *valere*, to be strong):
- Valentina #47 · Valerie #147 · Valentine (boys, rare)

**The *-hard* family** (from Germanic *hard*, brave/hardy):
- Everett #85 (Eberhard) · Leonard #674 (Leonhard, lion + hard) · Bernard #1,319 (bear + hard) · Conrad #470

In the Germanic tradition, *hard* (brave, hardy) was the most common second element in male compound names — giving us hundreds of names that carry the "brave" etymology.
