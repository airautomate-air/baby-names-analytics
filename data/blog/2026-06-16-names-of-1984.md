The year was 1984. Reagan won 49 states in November. The Cosby Show premiered and changed American television. Michael Jordan was drafted by the Chicago Bulls. About 3.7 million babies were born — the first year of a slow comeback from the late-70s baby bust — and their parents were largely giving them the same names their slightly older siblings had been given.

Here's what the SSA data shows for 1984.

## What stands out

**Jennifer's last year at #1.** Jennifer had been the most popular girls' name in America since 1970 — fourteen straight years at the top, which is the longest modern run of any name except Mary. In 1984 it had 50,563 babies. By 2024 it's at #547. The fall is as steep as the rise was dramatic. Jennifer is now almost exclusively a name for women in their 40s and 50s, with almost no current use.

**Jessica was ready to take over.** At #2 in 1984, Jessica would reach #1 in 1985 and hold it through the early 1990s. The Jennifer → Jessica transition looks obvious in hindsight — same long-vowel feel, same pop-culture saturation — but in 1984 it wasn't yet visible in the data.

**Crystal and Tiffany: the prime-time soap effect.** Crystal was #12 in 1984. Dynasty premiered in 1981 and its leading character Crystal Carrington (played by Linda Evans) drove a spike in the name. Tiffany at #15 would peak in 1988 — likely pushed by the jewellery brand, the Tiffany network positioning of CBS, and a general taste for aspirational gemstone and luxury-brand names that characterized the decade. Neither name is now used.

**Daniel is the surprise.** Daniel was #6 in 1984 and is #16 in 2024. It hasn't fallen — it's the best-performing name from the 1984 boys' list relative to its starting position. Biblical, two syllables, works in multiple languages: the Daniel formula is the same one that keeps David, Joseph, James, and John on the chart forty years later.

**Elizabeth stands apart.** In a girls' list where everyone else is either dead or declining, Elizabeth is at #17 in 2024. It was in the top 10 in 1984, and it's still in the top 20 now. Elizabeth is the most resilient girls' name in the American data — it has been somewhere between #7 and #25 continuously since the data begins.

**The biblical shift was just starting.** Joshua (#4) and Daniel (#6) in 1984 are early harbingers. By the mid-1990s, Joshua, Jacob, Matthew, Daniel, and Joseph would dominate the boys' list. The shift was a move away from mid-century generational names (Michael, Christopher, Jason, Brandon) toward biblical names with longer histories, which tend to wear better.
