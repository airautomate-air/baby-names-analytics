The year was 1944. D-Day — the Allied invasion of Normandy — happened on June 6. The Battle of the Bulge began in December. Millions of Americans were overseas; millions more were working in war industries. About 2.9 million babies were born, and the naming patterns of the early 1940s were shifting: James had finally overtaken John at the top of the boys' chart, and the girls' list was moving fast toward a set of names that would define a generation of American grandmothers.

Here's what the SSA data shows for 1944.

## What stands out

**The boys' list is remarkably durable.** Eight of the 15 are still inside the top 250 today. James and William are both back in the top 5. David is at #31 — barely any different from where it sat in 1944. The biblical and classic English names at the core of the 1944 boys' chart just don't age.

**Richard and Donald are the cautionary tales.** Richard (#5 in 1944) is now at #232. Donald (#8) is at #672 — a number that would almost certainly be lower without the political connotation of the last decade. Gary, which barely scraped the top 15 in 1944, is now at #1,130. These names don't have the classical roots to survive a generation of dormancy.

**The girls' list is almost uniformly dated.** Only Margaret (#209) and Judith (#326) are comfortably mid-chart in 2024. The rest are either rare or near-extinct. This is the starkest divergence between any boys' and girls' list in the decade-flashback series: the boys' names have aging; the girls' names have collapsed.

**Linda was about to happen.** Linda at #11 in 1944 is a name approaching its inflection point. Three years later it would overtake Mary and become the #1 girls' name in America — the start of a 1947–1952 run that briefly ended Mary's four-decade dominance. In 1944 it barely registers as a future phenomenon.

**Barbara's peak.** Barbara at #2 with 41,028 babies was at the very height of her moment. She'd been in the top 3 since the late 1930s. By 1960 she'd be falling fast. In 2024, Barbara is at #860 — a name with virtually no current use among the under-50s. It's one of the sharpest post-peak drops in naming history.

**What's next for these names?** The names best positioned for a 2030s revival from the 1944 list:
- **[Margaret](/name/margaret.html)** — already happening
- **[Dorothy](/name/dorothy.html)** — Wizard of Oz associations are now charming rather than dated
- **[Helen](/name/helen.html)** — clean, ancient, beginning to stir
- **[Judith](/name/judith.html)** — solid mid-chart position suggests quiet longevity
