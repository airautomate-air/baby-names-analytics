Music and naming have always been connected — the name Aria is literally an operatic solo. But the past twenty years have seen a new kind of music-inspired naming: not just names that mean music (Melody, Aria, Harmony) but the surnames of musicians themselves. Lennon, Hendrix, Marley, Jagger, and Bowie are all in the SSA data for 2024.

Here's the full picture.

## Musical concept names

**[Aria](/name/aria.html) — #26** (5,935 girls). An operatic solo; Italian *aria* (air). Aria's primary driver is *Pretty Little Liars* (see [pop culture names](/blog/pop-culture-names.html)) rather than opera, but it has a genuine musical meaning that reinforces its appeal. It's one of the most mainstream names in this entire list.

**[Melody](/name/melody.html) — #91** (2,771 girls). The succession of musical notes that forms a tune. Melody has been in the top 100 for over a decade. It's the most established of the musical concept names — clearer in meaning than Harmony and more concrete than Lyric.

**[Harmony](/name/harmony.html) — #264** (1,206 girls). The combination of simultaneously sounded notes; balanced agreement. Harmony is mid-chart and has been for twenty years. It's one of the virtue-style abstract names (see [virtue names](/blog/virtue-names.html)) that also has a musical meaning.

**[Lyric](/name/lyric.html) — #594** (507 girls; #1,363 for boys with 138 babies). The words of a song. Lyric is genuinely unisex and has been climbing for a decade. It's the music word that works as a name because it's also a category of poetry (lyric poetry) and a standalone English word.

**[Cadence](/name/cadence.html) — #1,035** (242 girls; #1,786 for boys). The rhythm of music or speech; a sequence of chords ending a musical phrase. Cadence is still niche but has been climbing. It has a military association too (cadence calls in training), giving it a dual appeal.

## Musician surname names

### Rock and roll

**[Lennon](/name/lennon.html) — #237** (1,311 girls; #783 for 320 boys). John Lennon (1940–1980), member of the Beatles. Lennon has been one of the most interesting surname-as-first-name stories of the past decade — it's now predominantly feminine in US data (1,311 girls vs 320 boys). Parents choosing Lennon for girls seems to be treating it as a sound-and-style choice rather than specifically honoring the Beatle. Still, Lennon's cultural standing as one of the most influential musicians of the 20th century gives the name genuine weight.

**[Hendrix](/name/hendrix.html) — #296** (1,130 boys; #3,086 for 52 girls). Jimi Hendrix (1942–1970). Hendrix is one of the clearest cases of a musician surname being used for a deliberate association — the name has essentially no traction as a given name historically, so parents using it are specifically invoking the guitarist. It's top 300 for boys and still climbing.

**[Marley](/name/marley.html) — #287** (1,100 girls; #1,235 for 161 boys). Bob Marley (1945–1981). Marley is the reggae icon name that has been climbing for over a decade. It's now more popular for girls than boys in US data. Jacob Marley from *A Christmas Carol* is the other association.

**[Presley](/name/presley.html) — #224** (1,373 girls; #1,729 for 95 boys). Elvis Presley (1935–1977). Presley has been climbing steadily and is now top 225 for girls. It functions primarily as a Southern American surname-style name rather than an explicit "this is for Elvis" choice, but the King of Rock and Roll is undeniably present.

**[Jagger](/name/jagger.html) — #846** (285 boys; #10,578 for girls). Mick Jagger (born 1943), lead singer of the Rolling Stones. Jagger is still primarily masculine and niche but has been climbing. It has a hard, angular quality — the double g — that gives it a distinctive sound.

**[Bowie](/name/bowie.html) — #1,206** (167 boys; #1,995 for 98 girls). David Bowie (1947–2016). Bowie has been climbing since Bowie's death in January 2016 — the name jumped significantly in 2016 and 2017 as tributes multiplied. The character of Davy Crockett's companion Jim Bowie (and the Bowie knife) gives the name an American frontier quality alongside the glam rock one.

**[Axl](/name/axl.html) — #1,028** (216 boys). Axl Rose (born William Bruce Rose Jr., 1962), lead singer of Guns N' Roses. Axl is the unusual spelling that makes the name immediately identifiable as an homage to the GN'R singer. It's mid-chart and holding.

**[Elvis](/name/elvis.html) — #1,170** (177 boys). Elvis Presley (1935–1977). The King's own given name is still in use — 177 boys per year, #1,170. It peaked in the 1950s–60s and has been declining but never fully disappeared.

### Other musicians

**Cobain** — Kurt Cobain (1967–1994). Only 6 boys per year in 2024. It's technically in the SSA data but extremely rare.

**[Morrison](/name/morrison.html) — #3,606** (31 boys). Jim Morrison (1943–1971) or Toni Morrison (1931–2019). Morrison as a first name is still very rare.

**Bono** — Not in SSA data with meaningful numbers. The U2 singer's name hasn't crossed over.

## The music naming pattern

**Death accelerates adoption.** Bowie jumped after 2016. Lennon's climb has been consistent since Yoko Ono's continued presence kept the name visible. Musicians who died at their peak (Hendrix, Cobain) or who have strong ongoing cultural presence (Lennon, Marley) have the most naming traction.

**Surnames work better than first names.** Elvis (#1,170) is used far less than Presley (#224). Jagger (#846) outpaces Mick. The surname has more distance from the specific person and works more naturally as a given name in English (where surname-as-given-name is an established tradition).

**Girls adopt rock surnames more readily.** Lennon, Marley, Presley — all more common for girls than boys. The surname-as-first-name trend has been more robust for girls since 2000 (Kennedy, Madison, Harper, Logan), so rock surnames fit that current.

**The music must be timeless.** Hendrix (#296) is a more consistent choice than Cobain (#11,271). Marley (#287) far outpaces Morrison (#3,606). The music has to have maintained cultural presence across generations — names reflecting flash-in-the-pan moments don't hold.

---

## Musical concept names vs. musician names

The two categories are different in tone. Musical concept names (Melody, Aria, Harmony, Lyric) are aspirational — they're wishing a musical quality onto the child. Musician surname names (Lennon, Hendrix, Marley) are tribute names — they honor a specific artist.

The musical concept names are broader in appeal and less tied to a specific era. Aria at #26 is broadly mainstream. Melody at #91 has been stable for decades. The musician surname names are more specific in what they signal about the parents' cultural tastes — which may limit their ceiling but keeps them distinctive.
