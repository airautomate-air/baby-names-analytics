If fire names evoke passion and transformation (see [names-meaning-fire](/blog/names-meaning-fire.html)), earth names evoke something different: stability, rootedness, nurturing, the ground beneath your feet.

Sage. Eden. Clay. Terra. Reed. Fern. Sable. Slate. These names connect their bearers to the natural world in its most literal, physical sense. And they have been rising steadily in American naming as parents seek names that feel grounded, organic, and real.

## Eden: the garden name

**[Eden](/name/eden.html)** — Girls #136, 2,700 babies in 2024.

Eden is the biblical garden of creation — a paradise of perfect nature before the Fall. As a name, it carries both the earth/nature meaning and the paradise/spiritual meaning.

| Year | Rank | Babies |
|------|------|--------|
| 1994 | ~#700 | ~308 |
| 2004 | #404 | 771 |
| 2010 | #228 | 1,483 |
| 2014 | #152 | 2,222 |
| 2018 | #136 | 2,682 |
| 2022 | #130 | 2,787 |
| 2024 | **#136** | **2,700** |

Eden has been in the #130-#160 range for about a decade — steady and settled. It works across religious traditions (biblical for Christians and Jews, simply a beautiful word for secular parents) and has a melodic quality that belies its two-syllable brevity. The "-den" ending connects it to names like Hayden, Aiden, and Eden's twin-in-sound, Ethan.

## Sage: the herb of wisdom

**[Sage](/name/sage.html)** — Girls #213, 1,644 babies; Boys #469, 658 babies in 2024.

Sage is simultaneously an herb (used for cooking and for cleansing in Indigenous traditions), an adjective meaning wise, and a word with strong spiritual/wellness connotations in contemporary culture. The rise of smudging, herbal medicine, and plant-based wellness in mainstream culture has brought Sage from the hippie fringe to the baby name mainstream.

| Year | Girls Rank | Boys Rank |
|------|-----------|----------|
| 1994 | ~#1,400 | ~#1,100 |
| 2004 | #652 | #617 |
| 2010 | #443 | #627 |
| 2014 | #352 | #571 |
| 2018 | #259 | #523 |
| 2022 | #213 | #487 |
| 2024 | **#213** | **#469** |

Sage is essentially flat in the low 200s for girls after a decade of rising. For boys it's declining slightly from a peak around 2018. The gender shift has been notable — Sage has become more female-coded over time, even as it was more gender-balanced in earlier data. See [gender-shift-names](/blog/gender-shift-names.html).

## Clay: the potter's material

**[Clay](/name/clay.html)** — Boys #466, 636 babies in 2024.

Clay is a word-name that connects to earth in the most direct sense: the material from which pottery is made, from which the first humans were shaped (in multiple mythological traditions), and which gives the land its color and texture.

| Year | Rank | Babies |
|------|------|--------|
| 1994 | #253 | 1,212 |
| 2004 | #314 | 1,041 |
| 2014 | #401 | 797 |
| 2020 | #434 | 721 |
| 2024 | **#466** | **636** |

Clay is declining from its 1990s peak. It has a strong country-Western undertone (Garth Brooks's given name is Troyal Garth; Clay Walker is a country singer) that may be limiting its appeal outside of rural and Southern naming traditions. See [country-western-names](/blog/country-western-names.html).

## Fern: the great Victorian return

**[Fern](/name/fern.html)** — Girls #752, 390 babies in 2024.

Fern was a Victorian botanical name that peaked in the early 1900s and then nearly disappeared from American naming. It's now making a vintage comeback.

| Year | Rank | Babies |
|------|------|--------|
| 1920 | ~#80 | ~3,200 |
| 1960 | ~#500 | ~560 |
| 1994 | ~#1,800 | ~60 |
| 2010 | ~#1,700 | ~78 |
| 2014 | ~#1,600 | ~85 |
| 2020 | #1,008 | 238 |
| 2024 | **#752** | **390** |

Fern crossed back into the top 1,000 recently after decades outside it. *Charlotte's Web* (E.B. White, 1952) kept Fern in cultural memory — Fern is the girl who saves Wilbur the pig. The book is still read by every generation of elementary schoolers, giving Fern a consistent gentle cultural exposure. See [skip-generation-names](/blog/skip-generation-names.html) for the broader Victorian comeback pattern.

## Briar and Stone: the new earth names

**[Briar](/name/briar.html)** — Girls #399, 852 babies in 2024.

Briar means a thorny bush or shrub — the wild rose briar, the briar patch. It's a fairy tale word (Sleeping Beauty is sometimes called Briarrose) that has become a modern botanical name. Rising from #667 (2014) to #399 (2024) is a meaningful 30-year decade climb.

**[Stone](/name/stone.html)** — Boys #718, 412 babies in 2024.

Stone is a pure word-name meaning rock or stone. It has the tough, minimal aesthetic that characterizes the Old Money/preppy naming wave (see [preppy-names](/blog/preppy-names.html)) and also connotes solidity and permanence. The Oliver Stone association gave it some cultural familiarity, and it's been rising from deep in the SSA ranks since 2010.

## Why earth names now?

The rise of earth names tracks with several converging cultural forces:

**1. Environmental consciousness.** A generation of parents concerned about climate change and environmental degradation are choosing names that honor the natural world.

**2. Wellness culture.** Sage for smudging, clay for skincare, fern for interior decoration — these are all wellness-culture touchstones that have crossed from practice into naming.

**3. Reaction to digital life.** In an increasingly screen-mediated world, names that evoke physical, tangible, natural things feel grounded and authentic.

**4. Aesthetic minimalism.** Clay, Fern, Reed, Stone — these are short, clean names that resist elaboration. They fit the minimalist aesthetic that dominates contemporary design and increasingly, naming.
