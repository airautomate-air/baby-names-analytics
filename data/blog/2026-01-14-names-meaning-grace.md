"Grace" in English naming comes from two entirely separate traditions: the Hebrew *chanan* (grace, favor, God's gift of kindness) and the Latin *gratia* (grace, elegance, gratitude). Both have produced major naming clusters — and together, they account for more popular names than almost any other single meaning.

The biggest surprise: John (#21 for boys) is a grace-name. The Hebrew name *Yochanan* means "Yahweh is gracious" — and John, with all its variants across twenty languages, is the most widely distributed "grace" name in history.

## The Latin *gratia* family: Grace names

### Grace — #40 (5,002 babies)

**[Grace](/name/grace.html)** — from Latin *gratia*, the noun for grace, favor, elegance, and gratitude. Grace Kelly (Princess of Monaco) made this the definitive elegant name of the mid-20th century. After a mid-century decline, it's been solidly inside the top 50 since 2000.

Grace is the only name in this group where the English word and the name are identical — wearing its meaning on its face, which is increasingly rare in contemporary naming.

### Gracie — #248 (1,273 babies)

**[Gracie](/name/gracie.html)** — informal diminutive of Grace. Gracie Allen (comedian, of Burns and Allen). At #248, Gracie is climbing as part of the broader nickname-name trend. See [nickname names](/blog/nickname-names.html).

## The Hebrew *chanan* family: Favor/Grace names

### Hannah — #52 (4,054 babies)

**[Hannah](/name/hannah.html)** — Hebrew *Channah*: from the root *chanan*, to be gracious, to show favor. Hannah is the Old Testament mother of Samuel — she prays fervently for a child and God "shows her grace" (*chanan*). At #52, Hannah has been steadily popular for twenty years.

### Anna — #94 (2,700 babies)

**[Anna](/name/anna.html)** — the Latin/Greek form of Hannah. Anna carries the same *chanan* root through the Greek adaptation of the Hebrew name. In Christian tradition, Anna is the mother of the Virgin Mary (from apocryphal tradition). At #94.

### Gianna — #23 (6,097 babies)

**[Gianna](/name/gianna.html)** — Italian feminine form of Giovanni (John). Through the Giovanni/John pathway, Gianna carries the *Yochanan* ("Yahweh is gracious") root. Gianna Bryant (Kobe Bryant's daughter) significantly accelerated this name: it was at #247 in 2019 and reached #59 in 2020 after her death. Now at #23. See [timeless names](/blog/timeless-names.html).

### Joanna — #329 (942 babies)

**[Joanna](/name/joanna.html)** — directly from Hebrew *Yochana* (the feminine form of Yochanan/John): "Yahweh is gracious." Joanna of Castile (Joanna the Mad), Joanna Newsom. At #329.

### Annie — #192 (1,564 babies)

**[Annie](/name/annie.html)** — informal form of Anne/Anna, from Hannah. *Annie* (the musical, 1977) kept the name warm and American; Little Orphan Annie. At #192 and climbing through the nickname-name trend.

## The John family: Grace names for boys

John is the most remarkable grace-name because its meaning — "Yahweh is gracious" — has been completely obscured by its ubiquity. Every form of John across every language carries the same Hebrew root:

### John — #21 (8,047 babies)

**[John](/name/john.html)** — Hebrew *Yochanan*: *Yah* (Yahweh) + *chanan* (to be gracious): "Yahweh is gracious." John the Baptist and John the Apostle are the primary biblical bearers. John has been the most common given name in the English-speaking world for approximately 600 years. It has been in the American top 25 in every decade since SSA records began in 1880. At #21.

### Ian — #75 (4,547 babies)

**[Ian](/name/ian.html)** — Scottish Gaelic form of John. Ian Fleming, Ian McKellen, Ian Curtis. At #75, Ian is the highest-ranked non-English John variant. See [Scottish names](/blog/scottish-origin-names.html).

### Jonathan — #83 (4,018 babies)

**[Jonathan](/name/jonathan.html)** — Hebrew *Yonatan*: *Yah* + *natan* (gave): "Yahweh has given." Note: Jonathan comes from *natan* (gave), not *chanan* (gracious) — it's a cousin meaning not identical to John. But it's in the same Hebrew/biblical family.

### Nathan — #62 (5,010 babies)

**[Nathan](/name/nathan.html)** — Hebrew *Natan*, "he gave" (from *natan*). The prophet Nathan who named Solomon "Jedidiah." Like Jonathan, Nathan is from *natan* (gave) rather than *chanan* (gracious) — a gift-of-God name rather than a grace-name.

### Giovanni — #122 (2,951 babies)

**[Giovanni](/name/giovanni.html)** — Italian form of John (*Yochanan*). Giovanni da Verrazano, Giovanni Bellini, Giovanni Ribisi. At #122, reflecting the Italian-name trend. See [Italian names](/blog/italian-origin-names.html).

### Evan — #143 (2,526 babies)

**[Evan](/name/evan.html)** — Welsh form of John. Evan Peters, Evan Rachel Wood, Evan Dando. At #143. The Welsh form is less obviously connected to John than Giovanni or Juan, which is why Evan often feels like an independent name.

### Juan — #137 (2,601 babies)

**[Juan](/name/juan.html)** — Spanish form of John. Juan is inside the top 150 reflecting the Spanish-speaking population in the US. At #137.

### Ivan — #153 (2,340 babies)

**[Ivan](/name/ivan.html)** — Slavic form of John. Ivan the Terrible; Ivan Turgenev; Ivan Drago (*Rocky IV*). At #153 and climbing, Ivan brings a Slavic literary quality to the same *Yochanan* root.

### Sean — #436 (726 babies)

**[Sean](/name/sean.html)** — Irish form of John (from Old Irish *Seán*, which came from Norman French *Jehan* → John). Sean Connery, Sean Penn, Sean Combs. At #436 — significantly lower than Ian, which is the other Celtic John form, despite Sean having a stronger celebrity profile.

## What grace means in naming

Parents choosing grace-names are choosing from two different theological traditions:

**Hebrew *chanan*** (favor, mercy, God's kindness toward humanity): Hannah, John, and all their variants carry the idea that grace is something received from God — an undeserved gift of divine favor. This connects to the major religious traditions of Western civilization.

**Latin *gratia*** (elegance, beauty, gratitude): Grace in the classical sense is aesthetic and social — the grace of movement, the grace of a dancer, the grace given in a prayer of thanks. Grace Kelly exemplifies this meaning.

Both traditions are active in current naming — and together, they make "grace" one of the most widely represented meanings in the American top 100.
