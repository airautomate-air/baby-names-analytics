Country music has always had a distinct relationship with names. The genre celebrates specific American identities — the frontier, the South, working-class dignity — and the names associated with it reflect that. In the last fifteen years, several country music names have moved from the cultural margins to the mainstream. Here's the data.

---

## Boys: Country and Western Names

### Wyatt — #38 (6,581 babies)

**[Wyatt](/name/wyatt.html)** is the dominant Western name of 2024 — #38 with 6,581 babies. Wyatt Earp is the most famous bearer; the name has been in the American frontier consciousness since the 1880s. Wyatt was at #567 as recently as 1980 and at #111 in 2000. Now at #38, it's approaching the mainstream.

What made Wyatt break through when other Western names didn't? The three-syllable feel, the strong ending, and the lack of any specific generational baggage. Wyatt was never overused — it was simply waiting.

---

### Waylon — #65 (4,878 babies)

**[Waylon](/name/waylon.html)** is the story of a legend's name coming back after his death.

| Year | Rank | Count |
|------|------|-------|
| 2001 | #1,265 | 105 |
| 2003 | #840 | 207 |
| 2007 | #520 | 502 |
| 2013 | #290 | 1,201 |
| 2019 | #113 | 3,438 |
| 2024 | **#65** | 4,878 |

Waylon Jennings died in February 2002. The jump from 105 babies in 2001 to 207 in 2003 tracks almost exactly with his death. This is the same pattern that affected Cash (see below), Lennon, and Bowie: a legend dies and parents name children after them, but unlike a pop-culture spike, this one *keeps climbing* rather than spiking and receding.

The sustained rise suggests that Waylon isn't just a tribute name — it's been absorbed into the broader "strong, old Western male name" category. Waylon Jennings was an Outlaw Country figure (he and Willie Nelson deliberately rejected Nashville's polished sound), which gives the name a rebellious quality that fits the current appetite for names with attitude.

---

### Cash — #316 (1,085 babies)

**[Cash](/name/cash.html)** is the other "death spike" story:

| Year | Rank | Count |
|------|------|-------|
| 2000 | #1,561 | 77 |
| 2004 | #793 | 228 |
| 2006 | #380 | 787 |
| 2008 | #254 | 1,388 |
| 2024 | #316 | 1,085 |

Johnny Cash died in September 2003. The trajectory is almost perfectly plotted: near-invisible in 2000, then a rapid climb from 2004 through 2008 as parents processed the loss. Unlike Waylon, Cash plateaued rather than keeping climbing — around #260–316 since 2008. This may be because Cash is a more common word (as in money), which limits its distinctiveness as a name.

See [music names](/blog/music-names.html) for more on how musician deaths affect naming.

### Beau — #69 (4,694 babies)

**[Beau](/name/beau.html)** — French for handsome (*beau*), with strong American Southern associations. Beau Bridges, Beau Biden (Joe Biden's late son), *True Blood*'s vampire-adjacent Beau Stackhouse. Beau was #203 in 1980, fell through the 1990s and 2000s, and is now at #69 — firmly inside the top 100.

The French spelling gives it a slightly more elegant quality than Bo (which is at #451), while preserving the Southern one-syllable sound.

---

### Rhett — #174 (2,134 babies)

**[Rhett](/name/rhett.html)** — from *Gone with the Wind*'s Rhett Butler (1936); also Thomas Rhett (country singer, born 1990). Rhett was at #704 in 1980 and is now at #174, with most of the climb happening after 2010. The double source — literary classic and contemporary country star — gives it unusual staying power.

---

### Colter — #218 (1,643 babies)

**[Colter](/name/colter.html)** — English occupational surname (a colt-keeper); also John Colter, the American mountain man. Colter Wall is a Canadian country/folk singer (born 1995) who has introduced the name to a younger generation through his deeply traditional sound. At #218, Colter is one of the fastest-climbing frontier names in current data.

---

### Hank — #425 (739 babies)

**[Hank](/name/hank.html)** — diminutive of Henry, but long used as a standalone given name in American tradition. Hank Williams (1923–1953) is the ur-country musician. At #425, Hank is climbing back from a mid-century low — it was #926 in 1980 and #1,490 in 2000. The vintage revival is reaching it.

---

### Colt — #276 (1,224 babies)

**[Colt](/name/colt.html)** — the young horse; also the Colt .45 revolver. Colt was at #5,031 in 1980 and began climbing steadily in the 2000s. At #276 in 2024, it has the same short, punchy Western quality as Jett, Nash, and Beau.

## Girls: Country and Western Names

### Lainey — #38 (5,147 babies)

**[Lainey](/name/lainey.html)** is the biggest country music naming story of 2024 — and one of the fastest single-name rises in recent SSA data.

| Year | Rank | Count |
|------|------|-------|
| 2015 | #576 | 531 |
| 2018 | #460 | 684 |
| 2021 | #252 | 1,227 |
| 2022 | #174 | 1,698 |
| 2023 | #55 | 3,937 |
| 2024 | **#38** | 5,147 |

The acceleration beginning in 2022–2023 tracks almost exactly with Lainey Wilson's rise. Wilson won CMA Entertainer of the Year for 2022 (announced November 2022) and 2023. Her album *Bell Bottom Country* (2022) and her starring role in *Yellowstone* brought her to national prominence. The jump from #576 in 2015 to #38 in 2024 — from 531 babies to 5,147 — is extraordinary.

Lainey was climbing before Wilson (it had been rising since at least 2015), but the last two years' acceleration is almost certainly her effect.

---

### Daisy — #76 (3,091 babies)

**[Daisy](/name/daisy.html)** — the flower; also *The Great Gatsby*'s Daisy Buchanan. Country music loves Daisy: Daisy May Cooper, the "Daisy Dukes" of Southern culture, *Dukes of Hazzard*'s Daisy Duke. At #76 in 2024, Daisy is approaching the top 50 — it was #418 in 1980 and has been climbing steadily for two decades.

---

### Jolene — #607 (493 babies)

**[Jolene](/name/jolene.html)** — Dolly Parton's 1973 song has made this one of the most famous names in country music. Despite the song's ubiquity, Jolene has never become mainstream as a baby name — it was #337 in 1980, fell to #1,294 in 2000, and is now climbing back to #607. The song's continued omnipresence (covered by everyone from Miley Cyrus to Pentatonix) keeps the name visible without overusing it.

---

### Georgia — #110 (2,458 babies)

**[Georgia](/name/georgia.html)** — the state; also Ray Charles' signature ("Georgia on My Mind"). Georgia is climbing as a place name with Southern associations. At #110 in 2024, it's one of the most mainstream Western-adjacent names on this list. See [place names](/blog/place-names.html).

---

### Loretta — #677 (422 babies)

**[Loretta](/name/loretta.html)** — Loretta Lynn, the Coal Miner's Daughter, is one of country music's greatest legends. The name peaked in the 1950s–1960s, fell to #2,052 in 2010, and is now at #677 — the beginning of a vintage revival that will likely accelerate.

---

### Willa — #423 (729 babies)

**[Willa](/name/willa.html)** — Germanic (from *willa*, will, determination). Willa Cather (the novelist); Willa Holland. At #3,502 in 1980, Willa was essentially unused. Now at #423 and climbing. It has the frontier/literary quality that fits the current taste for short vintage names.

---

## The Western sound profile

The names climbing in country and Western territory share a distinct acoustic profile:

**For boys:** Short (1–2 syllables), ending in a hard consonant or open vowel — Wyatt, Beau, Cash, Colt, Hank, Rhett. These are punchy names with physical weight.

**For girls:** Soft -ey or -ie endings, often flower/nature names — Lainey, Daisy, Jolene, Willa. The Western girls' naming tradition leans more feminine and lyrical than the boys'.

**Shared:** Strong Appalachian/Southern/frontier American cultural associations. These are not names imported from European or biblical traditions (though some have those origins) — they feel specifically American.

---

## The Yellowstone effect

The TV series *Yellowstone* (2018–present) and its spin-offs (*1883*, *1923*) have brought frontier/Western aesthetics to a national audience. Names from the show — Kayce, Rip, Beth, Elsa — are climbing in the data, though few have reached mainstream levels yet. The broader effect is to make Western-adjacent names feel contemporary and legitimate rather than specifically rural.
