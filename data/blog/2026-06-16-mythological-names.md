Classical mythology has always influenced English naming, but something unusual is happening right now: mythological names are not just surviving, they're dominating. Luna (#13), Aurora (#16), Penelope (#28), Nova (#39), Iris (#71) — five names from Greco-Roman mythology are in the top 75 girls' names in America. This is unprecedented in modern American naming history.

Here's the full picture of mythological names in 2024 US data.

## Girls: Mythological Names by Popularity

### Roman mythology

**[Luna](/name/luna.html) — #13** (7,135 babies). The Roman goddess of the moon; *luna* is the Latin word for moon. Luna has been one of the fastest-rising names in American history — from essentially unknown before 2000 to top 15 in 2024. Its Latin astronomical meaning is the source, but the Roman goddess predates the modern usage by 2,000 years.

**[Aurora](/name/aurora.html) — #16** (6,917 babies). The Roman goddess of dawn; *aurora* means "dawn" in Latin. The northern lights (aurora borealis) and the southern lights (aurora australis) take their names from her. In Disney's *Sleeping Beauty* she's Princess Aurora; the name has been climbing since 2015 partly through that association.

**[Nova](/name/nova.html) — #39** (5,044 babies). Latin *nova* (new); in astronomy, a nova is a star that suddenly brightens. While not strictly a deity's name, Nova comes from the same Roman astronomical-mythological vocabulary as Luna and Aurora. It's the third member of the celestial triumvirate dominating the girls' chart.

**[Diana](/name/diana.html) — #243** (1,293 babies). The Roman goddess of the hunt and the moon. Diana is the Roman equivalent of Artemis. Princess Diana (1961–1997) made the name famous in the modern era; in 2024 it's at #243. The mythological association and the royal association reinforce each other.

**[Flora](/name/flora.html) — #648** (452 babies). The Roman goddess of flowers and spring. Flora is the classic botanical goddess name — the source of *flora* (plant life) in English. Still niche but climbing through the vintage botanical revival.

**[Juno](/name/juno.html) — #1,382** (163 babies). The queen of the Roman gods; the 2007 film. Juno is one of the few goddess-title names being used. The film gave it a creative-indie-parent association.

### Greek mythology

**[Penelope](/name/penelope.html) — #28** (5,741 babies). Odysseus's faithful wife who waited twenty years for his return. Penelope is the Greek mythological name that has crossed most fully into mainstream modern use — it was top 30 in the 2020s with little sense of "this is a mythology name." Pennie, Penny, and Nell as short forms help. Its four syllables and three vowels give it the elaborate quality that current naming prefers.

**[Iris](/name/iris.html) — #71** (3,124 babies). The goddess of the rainbow; the flower; the part of the eye. Iris sits at a triple intersection — myth, nature, anatomy — that gives it unusual cultural depth. It's been climbing for fifteen years and may still have room.

**[Athena](/name/athena.html) — #90** (2,788 babies). The goddess of wisdom, craft, and warfare; the patron of Athens. Athena has been climbing rapidly — it was outside the top 200 in 2010 and is now top 90. The meaning (wisdom) is appealing; the sound is distinctive without being difficult.

**[Cora](/name/cora.html) — #102** (2,570 babies). From Greek *Korē* (maiden); another name for Persephone. Most parents choosing Cora in 2024 are probably not thinking of Persephone's maiden name, but the classical root is there. Cora feels like a vintage name rather than a mythology name to most modern ears.

**[Phoebe](/name/phoebe.html) — #183** (1,639 babies). An epithet of Artemis (the shining one) and a Titaness; also a New Testament deaconess. Phoebe is the name that bridges Greek mythology and Christian tradition through the same word. *Friends*' Phoebe Buffay kept it in the mainstream.

**[Daphne](/name/daphne.html) — #192** (1,564 babies). The nymph who was transformed into a laurel tree while fleeing Apollo. Daphne is one of the fastest-rising classical names of the past decade — it's now inside the top 200 and climbing. *Bridgerton*'s Daphne Bridgerton (2020) gave it a significant push.

**[Thea](/name/thea.html) — #348** (893 babies). Short form of Dorothea and Theodora; also the Titaness mother of the sun, moon, and dawn. Thea is in a similar position to Cora — most parents using it are thinking short-form-of-Theodora rather than Greek Titan, but the mythological root is present.

**[Lyra](/name/lyra.html) — #482** (638 babies). The lyre constellation named for Orpheus's instrument; Lyra Belacqua from *His Dark Materials*. Lyra combines an astronomical name (like Nova and Luna) with a mythological one. Still niche but growing.

**[Calliope](/name/calliope.html) — #499** (613 babies). The Muse of epic poetry; *kalos* (beautiful) + *ops* (voice). Calliope has the most elaborate sound of any mythological name in current use — five syllables, a double vowel, a name for the chief of the Muses. It's inside the top 500 and climbing.

**[Cassandra](/name/cassandra.html) — #613** (485 babies). The Trojan prophetess cursed to tell the truth but never be believed. Cassandra has a complicated story that doesn't discourage parents — the name is beautiful regardless of the myth, and "Cassandra complex" has given it a modern resonance.

**[Rhea](/name/rhea.html) — #616** (484 babies). The mother of the Olympians; a Titaness. Rhea has been climbing since around 2015 — part of the Greek mythology wave that has made Athena, Calliope, and Lyra more visible.

**[Selene](/name/selene.html) — #675** (424 babies). The Greek goddess of the moon (the equivalent of Luna). With Luna at #13, Selene at #675 shows how completely the Latin form has dominated over the Greek one.

**[Persephone](/name/persephone.html) — #737** (381 babies). The queen of the underworld; daughter of Demeter. Persephone is the most dramatic mythological name in current US use — five syllables, strongly associated with the underworld, beautiful sound. It was essentially unused before 2000 and is now approaching the top 700.

**[Artemis](/name/artemis.html) — #1,022** (246 babies). The goddess of the hunt and the moon; twin of Apollo. Artemis has been climbing slowly — it's a name that sounds bold and confident but remains niche. The NASA Artemis moon program has kept the name visible.

**[Ariadne](/name/ariadne.html) — #1,258** (184 babies). The Cretan princess who gave Theseus the thread to navigate the labyrinth. Ariadne is the mythological name that sounds most like a contemporary longer girls' name (cf. Adriana, Ariana). Still niche but growing.

## Boys: Mythological Names

**[Orion](/name/orion.html) — #325** (1,061 babies). The great hunter of Greek mythology; the most recognizable constellation. Orion has been inside the top 400 for over a decade and is slowly climbing. It's the mythological boys' name with the most mainstream traction.

**[Apollo](/name/apollo.html) — #414** (776 babies). The god of the sun, music, prophecy, and poetry. Apollo has broken into the top 500 for boys — a first for any Olympian deity name used as a first name. The NASA Apollo program gave it a technological-heroic quality alongside the mythological one.

**[Zephyr](/name/zephyr.html) — #1,129** (187 boys). The god of the west wind; personification of the gentle breeze. Zephyr is one of the most beautiful sounding boys' mythology names — three syllables, distinctive, easy to say. It's slowly climbing.

**[Perseus](/name/perseus.html) — #1,290** (152 boys). The hero who killed Medusa and saved Andromeda. Perseus is still quite rare but growing — it's a heroic mythological name that works as a first name without being overwhelming.

**[Mars](/name/mars.html) — #1,453** (124 boys). The Roman god of war. Mars is the planet, the candy bar, and the Olympian. It's still niche as a first name but climbing.

**[Achilles](/name/achilles.html) — #1,224** (164 boys). The greatest Greek warrior of the Trojan War. Achilles has a complex naming proposition — the "Achilles heel" idiom is the most widely known classical reference in English. Still, it's inside the top 1,250 and visible.

**[Eros](/name/eros.html) — #1,488** (121 boys). The god of love; Cupid in the Roman tradition. Eros is still rare as a first name, largely because of the direct meaning association. It's been slowly rising.

## Why mythology is winning

**Mythology names are exotic without being invented.** Luna, Aurora, and Athena feel distinctive but have 2,000-year track records. They're not made-up names; they're names that have just been less fashionable in recent decades.

**The astronomy connection.** The Roman mythology of the night sky — Luna, Aurora, Nova, Lyra, Orion, Perseus — resonates with the current interest in space. The James Webb Space Telescope's images have renewed popular interest in astronomy, and the names that map to astronomical objects are benefiting.

**Long vowels and -a endings.** Luna, Aurora, Athena, Penelope, Calliope, Lyra, Rhea, Selene, Ariadne — nearly all end in -a or have prominent long vowels. This fits the current sound preferences for girls' names exactly.

**Ancient roots feel modern.** Paradoxically, the most ancient names feel fresh precisely because they haven't been in common use for decades. Athena hasn't been overused in any living generation; it can be reclaimed without nostalgia.
