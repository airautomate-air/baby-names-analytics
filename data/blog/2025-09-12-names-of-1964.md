The year was 1964. The Beatles played Ed Sullivan in February and a generation of parents discovered British pop. The Civil Rights Act passed in July. President Kennedy had been dead seven months; Lyndon Johnson would win the November election in a landslide. And the baby boom was at its absolute apex — more than 4 million babies were born. The naming data from that year is striking on both sides: the boys' list is a fortress of durability, and the girls' list is almost entirely collapsed sixty years later.

Here's what the SSA data shows for 1964.

## What stands out

**Michael and John, side by side.** Michael had 82,619 births in 1964; John had 82,519. That's a gap of exactly 100 babies out of 4 million. No two names have ever been this close at the top of the chart. Michael held the #1 spot for boys for most of the period from 1954 to 1998 — but 1964 is the year it almost wasn't.

**The boys' list has aged remarkably well.** Nine of the top 15 boys' names from 1964 are still inside the top 250 today. James is back at #5. William is at #10. David, Joseph, and Thomas are all inside the top 40. The bedrock of the 1964 boys' chart is the same bedrock that's been there since 1914 — biblical names and English classics that have survived every trend.

**Jeffrey, Scott, and Kevin are the exceptions.** These three are the names that feel most purely of their era. Jeffrey is at #520; Scott at #565; Kevin at #196 (and falling fast). They don't have the classical roots to survive a generation of dormancy, and no cultural wave has arrived to push them back.

**Lisa's rise and fall.** Lisa was #1 in 1964 with 54,271 babies — and is now at #985. The arc is one of the steepest in the dataset. In the 1960s, Lisa spread from Italian-American communities to the broader US mainstream; by the mid-1970s it was everywhere; by the 1990s it was fading; today it's virtually a sealed time capsule of a name. "Lisa" now almost always means someone born between 1960 and 1975.

**The girls' list is a graveyard.** Only Kimberly (#246) and Laura (#359) are anywhere near the mid-chart. Mary (#132) persists on its biblical longevity alone. Susan, Karen, Patricia, Donna, Tammy, Pamela, Sandra, Lori — all of them are at or above #835. This is not a story of names cycling back; these are names that are functionally done.

**Tammy at #4,862.** The most extreme case: Tammy ranked #10 nationally in 1964 and is now at #4,862. That's rarer than almost any name you've never heard of. The name is so completely era-coded — Tammy Wynette's 1968 hit "Stand by Your Man" is the cultural peak — that no revival path is visible.

**The baby boom in numbers.** The 1964 top 15 boys' names alone account for approximately 754,000 births. In 2024, the top 15 boys' names account for fewer than 150,000 births from a much larger population. The concentration of the baby boom era is staggering — parents in 1964 were choosing from a much smaller pool of names, and choosing the same ones.
