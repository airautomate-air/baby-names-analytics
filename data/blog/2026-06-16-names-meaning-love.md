Names meaning love and "beloved" come from nearly every language family that has influenced English naming: Hebrew *dawid* (beloved), Latin *amare* (to love), Greek *philos* (love/friendship), Welsh *carys* (love), and Old French *aimée* (loved). Here's the complete picture with 2024 ranks.

---

## Girls Names Meaning Love and Beloved

### Amy — #228 (1,344 babies)

**[Amy](/name/amy.html)** is the most directly love-derived name in common American use. From Old French *aimée*, the feminine past participle of *aimer* (to love), which itself comes from Latin *amare* — to love. Amy means simply "loved."

Amy peaked in the 1970s and early 1980s — it was in the top 10 from 1968 through 1979. Now at #228, it carries the same generational association as Jennifer and Heather. Amy Winehouse gave the name one last high-profile moment (2006–2011); Amy Adams keeps it visible.

### Cara — #1,296 (178 babies)

**[Cara](/name/cara.html)** — Italian *cara* (dear, beloved) and Irish *cara* (friend, beloved). Both traditions converge on "dear/beloved." At #1,296, Cara is rare in the US — it's more common in Ireland and Italy. Cara Delevingne (the model) uses it. The -a ending fits the current preference for Italian-feminine sounds.

### Amara — #121 (2,288 babies)

**[Amara](/name/amara.html)** — from Igbo (eternal, grace), Hausa (eternal), and West African traditions generally; also sometimes from Arabic *amara* (eternal life) or Latin *amara* (bitter, ironic). Not strictly a love-name etymologically, but often cited alongside *amare* because of the phonetic similarity. At #121 and climbing fast — it's inside the top 125 and accelerating.

### Amanda — #496 (617 babies)

**[Amanda](/name/amanda.html)** — Latin gerundive of *amare*: *amandus/amanda* means "worthy of being loved" or "she who must be loved." Amanda was created or popularized in the Renaissance Latin tradition of gerundive names. It peaked in the late 1980s–early 1990s (top 10 from 1979 to 1994) and has fallen steadily since. At #496, it's in the same mid-century-adjacent zone as Ashley and Brittany.

### Arabella — #206 (1,476 babies)

**[Arabella](/name/arabella.html)** — possibly from Latin *amabilis* (lovable, worthy of love) via a medieval confusion with *arabella*. The etymology is disputed — it may also come from *orabilis* (easily moved by prayer). Either way, the *amabilis* reading gives it a love-meaning through the same *amare* root as Amanda and Amy. At #206.

### Davina — #648 (452 babies)

**[Davina](/name/davina.html)** — Scottish feminine form of David (beloved). Davina is rare in the US but common in Scotland. At #648.

## Boys Names Meaning Love and Beloved

### David — #31 (7,317 babies)

**[David](/name/david.html)** is the most famous name meaning "beloved." Hebrew *Dawid* (from *dod*, beloved or uncle). King David of Israel — shepherd, poet of the Psalms, slayer of Goliath — is the most influential historical bearer of any name in Western history. David has been in the American top 10 continuously from 1936 to 1993 (57 years), which is the longest run in SSA data after Mary. At #31 in 2024.

### Jedidiah — #809 (310 babies)

**[Jedidiah](/name/jedidiah.html)** — Hebrew *Yedidyah*: *yadid* (beloved/dear) + *Yah* (God) — "beloved of God." This was the secret name given to Solomon by the prophet Nathan (2 Samuel 12:25). Jedidiah is rare and biblical; the short form Jed is the American Western short form that has its own independent life.

### Philip — #521 (586 babies)

**[Philip](/name/philip.html)** — Greek *Philippos*: *philos* (love, friend) + *hippos* (horse) — "lover of horses." Philip is technically a love-name through the *philos* root: the same root as Philadelphia (brotherly love), philanthropy, philosophy (love of wisdom), and philology (love of words).

King Philip II of Macedon (father of Alexander the Great), Prince Philip (Duke of Edinburgh), Philip Glass, Philip Roth. At #521.

### Dawson — #139 (2,576 babies)

**[Dawson](/name/dawson.html)** — "son of David" (beloved's son). Dawson is the surname-as-first-name version of the David root. It was pushed into naming by *Dawson's Creek* (1998–2003), but has sustained well through the occupational-surname trend. At #139.

### Davis — #645 (429 babies)

**[Davis](/name/davis.html)** — "son of David." Davis is climbing as the cleaner, two-syllable alternative to Dawson. Bette Davis; Miles Davis. At #645.

### Amadeus — #1,284 (153 babies)

**[Amadeus](/name/amadeus.html)** — Latin *Amadeus*: *ama* (love, imperative of *amare*) + *Deus* (God) — "love God" or "one who loves God." Mozart's middle name (Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart); the 1984 film *Amadeus*. Rare at #1,284, but rising — it's the most elaborately beautiful name on this list.

## The *philos* (Greek love) family

Greek *philos* (love, friend) has produced a set of names:

| Name | Meaning | 2024 Rank |
|------|---------|-----------|
| **Philip** | lover of horses | Boys #521 |
| **Philadelphia** | brotherly love | (place name, not given name) |

The *-phile* suffix (*philos*) is more often used in compound words (Francophile, bibliophile) than in given names in English. But Philomena (Greek, "beloved friend"), Philbert, and Theophile all exist as rare given names.

## Names that mean love in other languages

### Welsh: Carys

**[Carys](/name/carys.html)** — Welsh *caru* (to love) + the feminine suffix *-ys*. Carys is a Welsh name invented or popularized in the 20th century; it's common in Wales and growing in English-speaking countries. Michael Douglas and Catherine Zeta-Jones named their daughter Carys in 2003. Very rare in US data.

### Hawaiian: Aloha

*Aloha* (love, peace, hello/goodbye in Hawaiian) is rarely used as a formal given name, though it appears occasionally.

### Japanese: Ai (愛)

Ai (love in Japanese) is sometimes used by Japanese-American families but doesn't appear prominently in the US data.

## Names that *look* like love but aren't

Several popular names are phonetically similar to love-words but have different etymologies:

**Amelia** (#3) — from Germanic *Amalia*, meaning "work/industry" (from *amal*). The *am-* beginning is coincidental; it's not related to *amare*.

**Amelie** — French diminutive of Amelia. Same Germanic work-root, not love.

**Amara** (#121) — West African, meaning eternal or grace; not etymologically from Latin *amare* despite the phonetic similarity.

**Romeo** (#283 for boys) — Italian, from the word for "pilgrim to Rome." Not love — though Shakespeare's *Romeo and Juliet* makes the name entirely associated with romantic love.

## The most overlooked love-name

**David** (#31) may be the most overlooked love-name in American culture. The name is so thoroughly associated with its biblical bearer that the underlying "beloved" meaning has become invisible. A child named David is named "beloved" — the same meaning as Amy, or Cara, or Jedidiah — but this is not how the name is currently understood.
