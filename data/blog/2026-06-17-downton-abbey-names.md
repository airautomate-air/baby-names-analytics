*Downton Abbey* premiered in 2010 and became the most-watched drama in British television history, drawing 12-15 million viewers per episode in the UK and millions more on PBS in America. Set in a Yorkshire country house between 1912 and 1926, it was essentially a masterclass in Edwardian and interwar names.

What it did to baby names is fascinating and underappreciated.

---

## The names of Downton

*Downton Abbey* had an unusually rich cast of named characters — upstairs, downstairs, and extended family. The core names:

**Upstairs (Crawley family):**
- Lady **Mary** Crawley (eldest daughter)
- Lady **Edith** Crawley (middle daughter)
- Lady **Sybil** Crawley (youngest daughter)
- **Cora**, Countess of Grantham (American mother)
- **Violet**, Dowager Countess (grandmother)
- **Robert**, Earl of Grantham (father)
- **Matthew** Crawley (heir)

**Downstairs:**
- **Carson** (butler)
- **Anna** (lady's maid)
- **Thomas** (footman, later butler)
- **Bates** (valet)

## Violet: the biggest winner

**[Violet](/name/violet.html)** — Girls #97, 4,362 babies in 2024.

The Dowager Countess Violet (played memorably by Maggie Smith) is arguably the most quoted TV character of the 2010s. And Violet the name has had a remarkable run.

| Year | Rank | Babies |
|------|------|--------|
| 2000 | #570 | 510 |
| 2005 | #278 | 1,200 |
| 2010 | #168 | 1,913 |
| 2014 | #116 | 3,103 |
| 2018 | #100 | 4,183 |
| 2022 | **#91** | **4,625** |
| 2024 | #97 | 4,362 |

Violet had already been rising before *Downton* — it was part of the broader Victorian flower name revival alongside Lily, Iris, and Rose. But the show accelerated it. The association with one of the most beloved characters in recent TV history — sharp, witty, imperious, ultimately loving — gave Violet an aspirational quality that name trends thrive on.

This is the same mechanism that made Penelope rise after *Bridgerton* (see [bridgerton-names](/blog/bridgerton-names.html)) — a name attached to a character who is both beloved and admirable.

## Cora: the American countess

**[Cora](/name/cora.html)** — Girls #120, 3,012 babies in 2024.

Cora Crawley is the American-born Countess of Grantham — gentle, wise, and usually right. Her name is a 19th-century American classic (from the Greek *Kore*, "maiden," a name for Persephone) that had been fading before the show.

| Year | Rank | Babies |
|------|------|--------|
| 2000 | #600 | 454 |
| 2008 | #483 | 627 |
| 2012 | #390 | 874 |
| 2015 | #249 | 1,487 |
| 2018 | #157 | 2,268 |
| 2022 | #121 | 2,988 |
| 2024 | **#120** | **3,012** |

Cora's rise is steady and still ongoing. It sits in a sweet spot: short enough to feel modern, classical enough to feel substantive, rare enough to feel distinctive. The -ora ending (Nora, Cora, Aurora, Dora) is currently popular (see [names-ending-in-a](/blog/names-ending-in-a.html) for the broader -a pattern).

## Mary: the perennial that just keeps declining

**[Mary](/name/mary.html)** — Girls #129, 2,902 babies in 2024.

Lady Mary Crawley is the complex, steely protagonist of the show. But Mary the name has been declining for 60 years, and not even a beloved TV protagonist has slowed it.

| Year | Rank | Babies |
|------|------|--------|
| 1954 | **#1** | 62,428 |
| 1974 | #10 | 20,184 |
| 1994 | #42 | 7,912 |
| 2004 | #78 | 5,062 |
| 2010 | #113 | 2,947 |
| 2014 | #124 | 2,671 |
| 2020 | #131 | 2,758 |
| 2024 | #129 | 2,902 |

Mary is holding roughly flat in the low 100s. Its long decline from #1 to #129 is one of the great stories in American naming — it held the #1 position for most of the first half of the 20th century and then fell steadily as its religious and cultural associations became associated with specific older generations. See [us-number-one-girls-history](/blog/us-number-one-girls-history.html) for the full Mary story.

## Edith: the slow comeback

**[Edith](/name/edith.html)** — Girls #617, 505 babies in 2024.

Lady Edith spent much of the show as the overlooked middle daughter. The name Edith spent most of the 20th century declining from its early peak. Now, finally, both seem to be getting their due.

| Year | Rank | Babies |
|------|------|--------|
| 1924 | **#7** | 10,200+ |
| 1954 | #95 | 3,210 |
| 1994 | ~#1,300 | ~130 |
| 2004 | ~#1,600 | ~75 |
| 2014 | ~#900 | ~232 |
| 2020 | #749 | 358 |
| 2024 | **#617** | **505** |

Edith is climbing. From near extinction in the 2000s to 500+ babies in 2024 — that's a real vintage revival, the same pattern as [Hazel, Pearl, and Clara](/blog/vintage-girl-names-comeback.html). It will probably continue climbing as the "old lady name" cycle turns toward the 1920s era (see [skip-generation-names](/blog/skip-generation-names.html)).

## Sybil: too unusual to break through

**[Sybil](/name/sybil.html)** — Girls ~#1,100, ~190 babies in 2024.

Lady Sybil was beloved by fans before her shocking death in Season 3. The character inspired genuine grief — and a small but real uptick in the name.

| Year | Rank | Babies |
|------|------|--------|
| 2010 | ~#1,600 | ~75 |
| 2013 | ~#1,250 | ~140 |
| 2016 | ~#1,150 | ~175 |
| 2024 | ~#1,100 | ~190 |

Sybil is rising but has never cracked the top 1,000. The name has two challenges: the psychological association (the 1973 book and 1976 film *Sybil* about a woman with multiple personalities) and simply being unfamiliar enough that most parents encounter the spelling and pronunciation together for the first time.

## The broader Downton effect

*Downton Abbey* did something subtle but important for American naming: it made Edwardian and interwar names feel aspirational rather than just old. Before the show, names like Edith, Violet, Cora, and Sybil were grandmotherly in a negative sense — too old, too dusty. After the show, they became vintage in a positive sense — evocative, elegant, storied.

The show ran from 2010 to 2015, with a film in 2019. The naming effects were distributed over a decade, which is why they're harder to see in a single year of SSA data. Violet's rise from #344 (2008) to #97 (2024) is the clearest signal.

See [vintage-girl-names-comeback](/blog/vintage-girl-names-comeback.html) for the full picture of Edwardian revival names, and [bridgerton-names](/blog/bridgerton-names.html) for comparison to another period drama's naming impact.
