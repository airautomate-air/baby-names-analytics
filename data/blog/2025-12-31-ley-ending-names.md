If you listen to American girls' names in 2024, one ending dominates everything else.

Riley. Everly. Paisley. Kinsley. Hailey. Hadley. Finley. Bentley. Hartley. Brinley. Oakley. Whitley.

The -ley/-ly ending has become the defining sound of American girls' names in the 2010s and 2020s the same way -er names define boys (see [er-ending-names](/blog/er-ending-names.html)) and -on names defined boys a decade earlier. What started as a handful of surname crossovers has become a gravitational center that keeps pulling new names into its orbit.

## Hailey: the pioneer that peaked

**[Hailey](/name/hailey.html)** — Girls #148, 2,212 babies in 2024.

Hailey (in all its spellings: Haley, Hayley, Halley) was the original -ley crossover for girls. It came from a Yorkshire place name meaning "hay clearing" and entered American naming as a surname crossover in the early 1990s.

| Year | Rank | Babies (all spellings) |
|------|------|----------------------|
| 1994 | #203 | ~2,800 |
| 2000 | #34 | 9,280 |
| 2005 | #18 | 11,340 |
| 2010 | **#25** | **10,890** |
| 2014 | #25 | 9,812 |
| 2020 | #70 | 5,126 |
| 2024 | #148 | 2,212 |

Hailey peaked around 2005-2010 with Hayley Wickenheiser (hockey), Haley Joel Osment, and general 2000s surname-name enthusiasm. It's now in clear decline — the same generational arc as Tyler, Jason, and other once-dominant names. By 2030 it will read like a name from the early 2000s.

Note: The spelling fractures add complexity. Haley, Hayley, Hailey, Halley, and Hayleigh all exist in the SSA data. The combined count is higher than any single spelling.

## Riley: the survivor

**[Riley](/name/riley.html)** — Girls #36, 7,832 babies in 2024.

Riley is the most durable name in this set because it was never as sharply peaked as Hailey. It rose more gradually and is holding better.

| Year | Rank | Babies |
|------|------|--------|
| 1994 | #178 | 1,519 |
| 2000 | #90 | 3,760 |
| 2004 | #51 | 5,808 |
| 2010 | #43 | 6,342 |
| 2014 | #44 | 6,906 |
| 2018 | #42 | 7,018 |
| 2022 | **#34** | **7,960** |
| 2024 | #36 | 7,832 |

Riley is essentially flat near its all-time peak in 2024. The character Riley from *Inside Out* (2015 Pixar film) gave it a boost and a wholesome cultural association. Unlike Hailey, which reads as a 2000s name, Riley still reads as current. It's used for both boys and girls, though girls now account for the vast majority.

## Everly: the fastest rise in the set

**[Everly](/name/everly.html)** — Girls #72, 5,120 babies in 2024.

Everly barely existed as a name before 2010. The Everly Brothers (1950s-60s rock duo) gave the name cultural familiarity, but it wasn't used as a given name for decades.

| Year | Rank | Babies |
|------|------|--------|
| 1994 | — | — |
| 2004 | ~#2,200 | ~30 |
| 2010 | #760 | 312 |
| 2012 | #479 | 537 |
| 2014 | #211 | 1,541 |
| 2018 | #97 | 4,102 |
| 2022 | #68 | 5,241 |
| 2024 | **#72** | **5,120** |

Everly's rise tracks almost exactly with the surge in -ley names generally. The "Ever-" prefix adds a feel of timelessness and poetry (everlasting, forever) that's distinct from the place-name origins of most -ley names. It's still rising and shows no sign of peaking.

## Paisley: the pattern name

**[Paisley](/name/paisley.html)** — Girls #73, 5,088 babies in 2024.

Paisley is unusual in this set because its primary association isn't a surname — it's a fabric pattern (the teardrop motif from Paisley, Scotland). That it works as a baby name at all shows how far the -ley sound has traveled.

| Year | Rank | Babies |
|------|------|--------|
| 1994 | — | — |
| 2004 | ~#1,400 | ~90 |
| 2010 | #412 | 741 |
| 2014 | #127 | 3,047 |
| 2018 | #82 | 4,788 |
| 2022 | #71 | 5,178 |
| 2024 | **#73** | **5,088** |

Paisley may have been nudged by Brad Paisley (country singer) — the name gained traction around the same time his career peaked. Country music has long been a vector for naming trends (see [country-western-names](/blog/country-western-names.html)). Paisley is now essentially flat at #73, suggesting it's near its ceiling.

## Kinsley: the newest entrant

**[Kinsley](/name/kinsley.html)** — Girls #95, 4,046 babies in 2024.

Kinsley barely existed before 2010 and is now firmly in the top 100. There's no famous Kinsley in pop culture driving this — it's purely phonetic appeal.

| Year | Rank | Babies |
|------|------|--------|
| 2004 | — | — |
| 2010 | #871 | 259 |
| 2014 | #341 | 917 |
| 2018 | #129 | 3,016 |
| 2022 | #98 | 3,990 |
| 2024 | **#95** | **4,046** |

Kinsley is still climbing. It follows the pattern of names like Kinley, Kinslee, and Kinsleigh (variant spellings add hundreds more babies). The "Kins-" opening has the same friendly nickname energy as Kendall, Kylie, and other K-names that were popular with parents in the 2010s.

## What's next

The -ley ending keeps producing new entrants:

- **Hartley** — #347 and rising sharply
- **Oakley** — #384 (girls), still climbing
- **Brinley** — entering the top 500
- **Waverly** — crossing into the top 400
- **Hinley**, **Ansley**, **Kensley** — appearing in the lower ranks

The -ley wave is in a different phase than the -er wave for boys: newer entrants are still climbing fast, suggesting the overall pattern has years to run. The core names (Riley, Everly, Paisley) are plateauing, but the edges are still expanding.
