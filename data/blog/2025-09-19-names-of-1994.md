## What stands out

**Michael's long goodbye.** By 1994 Michael had been America's #1 boy name for most of 40 years. It was already starting to slip — it would hold #1 until 1998, then hand off to Jacob. In 1994 it still had 54,000 babies; in 2024 it has around 10,000. The count is down 80%, but the rank is still #18. That's how saturated the name is across living generations — nearly every American knows multiple Michaels.

**The 1990s girls' list is even more date-stamped than the boys'.** Jessica was the Jennifer of this generation — enormous in the moment, nearly ungivable today. Brittany at #6 has collapsed to #2,247. Ashley, Megan, Kayla, Amanda — all firmly locked to a birth-year decade. The girls' list is naming's equivalent of a yearbook photo.

**Emily and Hannah are the survivors.** Of the 1994 top 15 girls, only Emily (#20) and Hannah (#41) are still in the modern top 50. Both are biblical or classical; both have 300+ years of continuous use behind them. That depth is the single best predictor of longevity.

**Tyler and Brandon didn't make it.** These were the decade-specific boys' hits — surname-names that felt fresh in 1993 and dated by 2005. Jacob, which crept into the 1994 top 10 at #7, would become #1 by 1999 and rule for 14 years. Looking back, the 1994 chart is showing the biblical revival in real time: Jacob and Joshua both rising, Tyler and Brandon near their peaks.

**Names from 1994 that might come back:**
- **[Rachel](/name/rachel.html)** — biblical, one syllable, a strong short form (Rach); already showing signs of quiet revival
- **[Lauren](/name/lauren.html)** — clean, strong, cross-generational; holding better than its cohort
- **[Hannah](/name/hannah.html)** — already made it through; never really left
- **[Sarah](/name/sarah.html)** — same; the biblical names don't fully leave
