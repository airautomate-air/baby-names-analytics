Names meaning light, bright, and dawn cluster near the top of almost every naming list in 2024. Eight of the top 25 girls' names and four of the top 25 boys' names have roots in light, brightness, or dawn. Here's the full picture with etymology and current ranks.

---

## Girls Names Meaning Light

### Eleanor — #14 (7,127 babies)

**[Eleanor](/name/eleanor.html)** — The etymology is disputed, but most sources trace it to the Provençal name *Aliénor*, which was influenced by the Greek *elenē* (torch, or light) and possibly the Hebrew *El* (God). Eleanor of Aquitaine, Eleanor Roosevelt. The name means "bright, shining light" in its Greek root. At #14, it's one of the most popular name in the current data with a luminous etymology.

Short forms: Ellie (#21), Nell, Nora (#22 — from the Irish form Eleanor → Nóra).

### Aurora — #16 (6,917 babies)

**[Aurora](/name/aurora.html)** — Latin for *dawn* — the first light of day. Aurora is the Roman goddess of the dawn; the aurora borealis is named for her. At #16, Aurora is the most explicitly celestial name in the top 20. See [mythological names](/blog/mythological-names.html).

### Ellie — #21 (6,306 babies)

**[Ellie](/name/ellie.html)** — short form of Eleanor (see above). At #21 as a standalone given name, Ellie is now more popular than most of its formal versions — except Eleanor itself. See [nickname names](/blog/nickname-names.html).

### Nora — #22 (6,128 babies)

**[Nora](/name/nora.html)** — Irish diminutive of Honora or Eleanor. Through the Eleanor → Nóra pathway, Nora carries the *elenē* (light) root. At #22.

### Lucy — #34 (5,396 babies)

**[Lucy](/name/lucy.html)** — English form of Lucia, from Latin *lux* (light). Lucy is one of the most direct light-names: the root word is simply *light*. Saint Lucia of Syracuse (the patron saint of the blind) is celebrated with light festivals in Scandinavian tradition. At #34, Lucy is near its historical high.

### Elena — #45 (4,567 babies)

**[Elena](/name/elena.html)** — Spanish and Italian form of Helen, from Greek *helenē* (torch, or moon/bright). Elena is the Romance-language version of Helen, currently at #45 — far more popular than Helen itself (#424).

### Claire — #67 (3,329 babies)

**[Claire](/name/claire.html)** — French form of Clara, from Latin *clarus* (clear, bright, famous). At #67.

### Clara — #78 (3,060 babies)

**[Clara](/name/clara.html)** — directly from Latin *clarus* (bright, clear). Saint Clare of Assisi (the founder of the Poor Clares religious order) is the most famous historical bearer. Clara Schumann, Clara Bow, the *Nutcracker*'s Clara. At #78 and climbing.

### Lucia — #98 (2,658 babies)

**[Lucia](/name/lucia.html)** — Italian form of Lucy, directly from *lux*. The Swedish Santa Lucia tradition involves a girl crowned with candles to bring light during the darkest season. At #98.

### Phoebe — #183 (1,639 babies)

**[Phoebe](/name/phoebe.html)** — Greek *Phoibe*, feminine of *Phoibos* (bright, pure, radiant). Phoebe is a Titaness associated with the moon and with prophetic light; Apollo was also called Phoebus (the radiant one). *Friends*' Phoebe Buffay helped push this back into use. At #183.

### Lucille — #274 (1,151 babies)

**[Lucille](/name/lucille.html)** — French form of Lucia. Lucille Ball; Lucille (the name of B.B. King's guitar). At #274 — the most formal of the Lucia family.

### Lena — #263 (1,211 babies)

**[Lena](/name/lena.html)** — short form of Helena, Magdalena, or Elena. Through the Helena/Elena pathway, shares the *helenē* light root. Lena Horne, Lena Waithe.

### Helen — #424 (726 babies)

**[Helen](/name/helen.html)** — the original Greek form (*helenē*) — possibly from *selene* (moon) or *helios* (sun), or from an older word meaning torch. Helen of Troy is the most famous bearer. At #424 — declining but still present. See [skip-generation names](/blog/skip-generation-names.html) for when Helen might revive.

## Boys Names Meaning Light

### Elijah — #8 (11,171 babies)

**[Elijah](/name/elijah.html)** — Hebrew *Eliyahu*: *El* (God) + *Yahu* (Yahweh, the LORD). The name means "my God is Yahweh" — with *El* (God) often connected to the root of *elah* (a tall tree, or lofty/brilliant). Elijah is the Hebrew prophet who was taken to heaven in a chariot of fire — the name is associated with divine fire and light. At #8.

### Lucas — #9 (10,703 babies)

**[Lucas](/name/lucas.html)** — from Latin *lux* (light); the Latinized form of Luke. Lucas has been in the top 10 for years. See [timeless names](/blog/timeless-names.html). The *Star Wars* connection (George Lucas) adds a sci-fi layer.

### Elias — #25 (7,653 babies)

**[Elias](/name/elias.html)** — the Greek form of Elijah. Same *El + Yahu* root — "my God is Yahweh" with the divine-fire association. Elias and Elijah are technically the same name in different languages; they're both climbing simultaneously.

### Luke — #34 (7,039 babies)

**[Luke](/name/luke.html)** — from Latin *lux* (light), via Greek *Loukas*. Saint Luke the Evangelist; Luke Skywalker. At #34, Luke is one of the most successful short-form light-names.

### Luca — #23 (7,959 babies)

**[Luca](/name/luca.html)** — Italian form of Luke, from *lux*. Luca is climbing faster than Luke — it has a Mediterranean quality that fits the current taste for Italian/Romance names. At #23.

### Eli — #92 (3,723 babies)

**[Eli](/name/eli.html)** — Hebrew, possibly from *elah* (lofty/oak) or as a short form of Elijah/Elias. Used in the Old Testament (Eli was a priest in the temple). Short, clean, and currently the most popular standalone short form of the Elijah cluster.

### Luciano — #348 (974 babies)

**[Luciano](/name/luciano.html)** — Italian form of Lucianus, from *lux*. Luciano Pavarotti. At #348 and climbing through the Italian-name trend.

### Uriel — #461 (670 babies)

**[Uriel](/name/uriel.html)** — Hebrew *Uriyel*: *Uri* (my flame/light) + *El* (God): "God is my light" or "flame of God." Uriel is one of the archangels in Jewish and Christian tradition — specifically associated with divine light. At #461, climbing.

## Why light names are dominant right now

Of the top 25 girls names in 2024, these have light-related etymology:
- **Eleanor** (#14) — torch
- **Aurora** (#16) — dawn
- **Ellie** (#21) — bright
- **Nora** (#22) — bright
- **Lucy** (#34) — light

Of the top 25 boys:
- **Elijah** (#8) — divine fire
- **Lucas** (#9) — light
- **Luca** (#23) — light
- **Elias** (#25) — divine fire
- **Luke** (#34) — light

This isn't coincidence. The preference for luminous, celestial-root names connects to a broader naming trend toward nature, cosmos, and positive meaning — the same impulse that's driving Luna (#13), Nova (#39), Aurora, and Orion (#323). See [celestial and mythological names](/blog/mythological-names.html).

Light has always been a positive symbol — across cultures, across religions, across history. Names meaning light signal brightness, hope, and warmth. In an era when many parents are choosing names deliberately for meaning rather than for fashion, the light etymology has unusual staying power.
